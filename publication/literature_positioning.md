# Literature Positioning for the P1-P100 Manuscript

This is an internal manuscript-development document. It records the closest conceptual and methodological neighbors and states what the present paper adds without claiming that neighboring work is deficient or superseded.

## 1. Computational and physical theories of consciousness

### Tegmark 2015

Max Tegmark, “Consciousness as a State of Matter,” *Chaos, Solitons & Fractals* 76 (2015), 238-270. DOI: 10.1016/j.chaos.2015.03.014.

**Role in this project:** conceptual origin for asking how physical subsystem structure, information, integration, independence, and dynamics could be connected to consciousness in a mathematically explicit way.

**Difference here:** the present manuscript does not propose a new state of matter or a preferred consciousness quantity. It asks whether an independently specified target factors through a declared physical or computational descriptor and develops falsification conditions for that claim.

### Integrated Information Theory

Larissa Albantakis et al., “Integrated information theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the properties of phenomenal existence in physical terms,” *PLOS Computational Biology* 19 (2023), e1011465. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465.

**Role:** major implementation-sensitive formal theory linking phenomenal claims to physical causal structure.

**Difference here:** the manuscript does not adopt IIT's axioms or identify an integrated-information quantity with consciousness. IIT can instead be treated as one possible source of a declared descriptor whose empirical sufficiency would need to survive independent target and model-family tests.

### Global Neuronal Workspace and Recurrent Processing

Representative references already in `references.bib` include Dehaene and Changeux (2011), Mashour et al. (2020), Changeux and Farisco (2026), and Lamme (2006).

**Role:** prominent functional, dynamical, and recurrent accounts with different commitments about access, broadcasting, recurrence, and implementation.

**Difference here:** the proposed framework is not another workspace or recurrence theory. It provides a common language in which a theory can declare the descriptor it regards as sufficient, after which factorization and residual target information can be tested.

## 2. The unfolding debate

### Doerig et al. 2019

Adrien Doerig, Aaron Schurger, Kathryn Hess, and Michael H. Herzog, “The unfolding argument: Why IIT and other causal structure theories cannot explain consciousness,” *Consciousness and Cognition* 72 (2019), 49-59. DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2019.04.002.

**Overlap:** both works focus on equivalence classes and the empirical burden of causal or implementation-sensitive consciousness claims.

**Difference:** the current paper does not assume that input-output equivalence settles the relevance of internal causal structure. Instead, it requires each theory to declare the descriptor whose equivalence classes are claimed to be consciousness-sufficient. If internal structure is included in the descriptor, it can be tested through the interventions and target contrasts that operationalize that structure. If it is excluded, target variation inside the resulting coarser equivalence classes becomes a direct challenge to sufficiency.

The manuscript should present the unfolding argument as an important challenge, not as an uncontested theorem about all causal-structure theories. Published responses dispute the scope of the required functional equivalences and emphasize temporal and perturbational structure. The manuscript's framework is designed to remain usable on either side of that dispute.

## 3. Negative and falsificatory methodologies

### Bridewell and Isaac 2021

Will Bridewell and Alistair M. C. Isaac, “Apophatic science: how computational modeling can explain consciousness,” *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2021(1), niab010. DOI: 10.1093/nc/niab010.

**Overlap:** this is a particularly close methodological neighbor. It argues that computational models can contribute negative knowledge about consciousness and explicitly aims to make methodological progress without deciding reductionist or nonreductionist metaphysics.

**What the present manuscript adds:**

1. an exact factorization criterion for what it means for a declared descriptor to be sufficient for an independent target;
2. a collision and conditional-information language for falsifying that sufficiency claim;
3. explicit target-provenance safeguards against constructing the target from the tested descriptor;
4. target-measurement channel identifiability and overidentifying adequacy tests;
5. certified distance from a complete continuous model family rather than validation of one fitted model;
6. finite-data continuation through dependence and drift;
7. selection-valid holdout and cross-fitted analysis;
8. e-value aggregation and anytime-valid sequential stopping.

The novelty claim should therefore be mathematical and inferential, not the broad idea that negative evidence can advance consciousness science.

## 4. Formal no-go methodology in consciousness science

### Kleiner and Ludwig 2024

Johannes Kleiner and Tim Ludwig, “The case for neurons: a no-go theorem for consciousness on a chip,” *Neuroscience of Consciousness* 2024(1), niae037. DOI: 10.1093/nc/niae037.

**Overlap:** both projects use formal no-go reasoning and emphasize that a theorem's conclusion is conditional on assumptions that must themselves be scientifically justified.

**Difference:** Kleiner and Ludwig derive a specific no-go conclusion for contemporary processing units under a dynamical-relevance assumption and chip-verification premises. The present manuscript is not a theorem that current AI hardware is conscious or nonconscious. It gives a general test architecture for any declared descriptor-target sufficiency claim and then builds statistical machinery for rejecting complete candidate families with controlled finite-data, selection, and stopping error.

This distinction should be explicit because both works use “no-go” reasoning but answer different questions.

## 5. Mathematical consciousness frameworks

### Kleiner 2019 and Kleiner-Tull 2020

These works formalize mathematical models of consciousness and the mathematical structure of IIT.

**Overlap:** commitment to explicit mathematical objects, maps, and structural relations rather than purely verbal theory comparison.

**Difference:** the current framework treats the consciousness-related target as operationally external to the declared descriptor and focuses on the falsifiability of a sufficiency map. It does not assume one preferred mathematical structure is already the phenomenal structure.

## 6. Adversarial theory testing

### Cogitate Consortium 2025

The Cogitate Consortium's adversarial comparison of Global Neuronal Workspace and Integrated Information Theory demonstrates the scientific value of precommitted contrasts between theories.

**Overlap:** emphasis on discriminating tests and protecting interpretation from post hoc flexibility.

**Difference:** the current manuscript supplies a mathematical layer beneath such comparisons. It distinguishes target validity, complete model-family separation, sampling uncertainty, selection validity, and sequential monitoring. It can therefore complement adversarial collaboration without depending on one pair of consciousness theories.

## 7. Building-block approaches

### Lopez and Wiese 2025

Azenet Lopez and Wanja Wiese, “Building blocks for theories of consciousness,” *Consciousness and Cognition* 134 (2025), 103919. DOI: 10.1016/j.concog.2025.103919.

**Overlap:** both approaches try to reduce premature commitment to whole theories and make comparison possible at the level of more elementary claims.

**Difference:** the present framework's elementary unit is a declared sufficiency relation among a descriptor, independently specified target, measurement channel, and candidate law. The paper then asks whether that unit survives exact and statistical falsification.

This recent building-block perspective is useful for explaining why the manuscript deliberately avoids ranking whole theories.

## 8. Empirical consciousness measures

Representative references in the repository include Casali et al. (2013), Mashour (2024), Maschke et al. (2024), Luppi et al. (2024, 2026), and other perturbational, dynamical, and information-theoretic work.

**Role:** demonstrate that consciousness science already contains operational targets and measurements that could eventually instantiate parts of the framework.

**Boundary:** the current manuscript does not reanalyze those datasets and should not imply that any cited empirical marker has already supplied the independent target required by P19-P100. Future empirical work must specify exactly what target is being measured and why its semantics are independent of the descriptor being tested.

## 9. Manuscript novelty statement

The safest strong novelty statement is:

> Existing work has developed mathematical theories of consciousness, challenges to causal-structure theories, negative computational methodologies, formal no-go arguments, and adversarial theory comparisons. The present contribution is an auditable inferential architecture that turns a declared physical or computational sufficiency claim into a factorization constraint and carries its possible rejection through independent target provenance, complete model-family separation, finite sampling, dependence, data-dependent selection, cross-fitted e-values, and anytime-valid sequential monitoring.

This statement should be preferred to claims that no previous work has mathematically studied consciousness, no-go theorems, computationalism, or model falsification.
