# Cover Letter Draft

Editors  
*Neuroscience of Consciousness*

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“Testing physical and computational sufficiency in consciousness science: exact model separation and anytime-valid falsification”** as a Research Article for the special issue **“Is There More to Consciousness Than Computation?”**.

The manuscript addresses the special issue's central question from a falsification and measurement perspective. Rather than assuming in advance that consciousness is determined either by computation alone or by a particular physical implementation, the paper asks what testable restrictions follow once a theory declares a computational, functional, neural, causal, quantum-operational, or richer physical descriptor to be sufficient for an independently specified consciousness-related target.

The core mathematical statement is a factorization criterion. For a declared descriptor \(T\) and independently defined target \(E\), deterministic sufficiency requires \(E=B\circ T\), while stochastic sufficiency requires the corresponding screening-off relation. This creates a theory-neutral way to identify exact collisions or residual target information that would falsify a declared sufficiency claim without treating that falsification as proof that consciousness is nonphysical.

The manuscript then develops the inferential conditions needed to make such a test scientifically usable. It addresses non-circular target construction, target-measurement robustness and adequacy, exact separation from a continuous latent model family, finite-sample rejection under dependence and predeclared drift, data-dependent model selection, cross-fitted certification, e-value aggregation, and anytime-valid sequential evidence. The worked model-family example is exact and synthetic by design. It demonstrates the mathematical and statistical machinery without presenting generated data as empirical evidence about consciousness.

A key contribution is the separation between three questions that are often conflated: whether a descriptor is mathematically sufficient for a target, whether the target and its measurement channel are independently defensible, and whether the observed data are strong enough to reject the complete declared model family with valid error control. The paper also makes the interpretation boundary explicit: rejection of a declared computational or physical descriptor does not establish nonphysical consciousness, and failure to reject does not validate a theory.

The work is accompanied by a public reproducibility record containing the complete theorem lineage, exact rational constructions, source-controlled figures, implementations, regression tests, equation provenance, and automated publication checks. The current scientific record contains 100 proposition-level results, with the journal manuscript consolidating the subset needed for one coherent argument rather than reproducing the repository chronologically.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. The associated repository is a public research record and code/proof archive rather than a previously published journal article. Any related public preprint produced from this manuscript will be disclosed in accordance with the journal's policy.

I believe the paper is particularly suited to this special issue because it does not attempt to settle computationalism versus implementationism by definition. Instead, it provides a common mathematical language for asking what each position would have to predict, how those predictions can be challenged by independent target measurements, and how evidence can remain valid through selection and sequential experimentation.

Thank you for considering this work.
