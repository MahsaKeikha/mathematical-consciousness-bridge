The whole research program in three stages

Find the physical system. Test whether its description is enough. Make the evidence measurable.

The stages answer different questions. Research I asks what physical subsystem is being tracked. Research II asks a direct sufficiency question: if two cases are the same according to the physical description a theory says is enough, must the independently measured target also be the same? Research III asks what observable evidence can justify about experiential targets under explicit uncertainty and dependence assumptions.

About the researcher · Where this question came from

The question that kept drawing me deeper.

My curiosity about consciousness began long before this repository. During my doctoral years, when I began research at TRIUMF, Canada's Particle Accelerator Centre and studied quantum mechanics, particle accelerators, measurement, and physical description more deeply, that curiosity became a scientific problem I could no longer treat only conceptually.

I wrote and completed a book about consciousness, human experience, physics, and questions raised by quantum mechanics, but I never published it. It always felt unfinished. What was missing was not another chapter; it was a mathematical layer strong enough to separate intuition from hypothesis, measurement from interpretation, and an interesting idea from a falsifiable scientific claim.

Years later, this research program is my return to some of those questions with formal definitions, identifiability conditions, sufficiency tests, measurement models, finite-data guarantees, counterexamples, and reproducible computation.

Today, as founder and CEO of Connected Care, the same scientific thread continues through my work on human biomarkers, physiological and behavioral signals, multimodal sensing, aging, neuroscience, cognitive change, and dementia. At both scales, the question is similar: what can an observable signal legitimately tell us about an underlying human state?

Research I · Physical subsystem identification

What physical system are we actually following through time?

Research I develops a moving-boundary observer mathematics. Its object is not “consciousness”; it is the physical subsystem itself. The program asks whether a candidate world-tube can be inferred from stochastic dynamics using integration, insulation, persistence, transport, global path optimization, identifiability, and finite-sample certification.

Research I pipeline

Measured observables → stochastic dynamics → candidate boundary → world-tube recovery

The operational question is whether one moving subsystem remains meaningfully identifiable while the measured coordinates carrying it change.

Research II · Bridge sufficiency and falsification

Does the chosen physical description really contain everything needed to account for the target?

Research II does not start by choosing an equation for consciousness. It starts with a claim that can be tested. A theory declares what physical or computational information should be sufficient, and the target is defined by an independent measurement route. The mathematics then asks whether the target truly depends only on that declared description, or whether some target-relevant distinction remains outside it.

1 · Declare

Say exactly what information the theory claims is enough

This may be a computational state, neural description, causal structure, quantum-operational state, or a richer physical descriptor. The claim is only tested relative to what is explicitly included.

2 · Measure independently

Define the target without building the answer into it

The target cannot simply be calculated from the same descriptor and then used as evidence that the descriptor explains it. Research II therefore tests target provenance, measurement noise, identifiability, and model adequacy.

3 · Try to falsify

Look for distinctions the declared description cannot account for

If two cases are equivalent under the declared description but the independently defined target differs in a way the model forbids, that description is insufficient for that target. With finite data, the program adds uncertainty and error-control guarantees before allowing rejection.

P19: the core sufficiency theorem

P19 states the exact deterministic and stochastic conditions under which a target can depend only on a declared physical descriptor. It also states what kind of collision or residual can refute that sufficiency claim.

P71-P95: make the test scientifically defensible

These results prevent circular targets, test noisy measurement models, separate complete model families from data, and carry exact incompatibility results into finite samples, dependence, and drift.

P96-P100: keep evidence valid while analysis adapts

P96 protects selected holdout certification, P97 controls simultaneous candidate-family selection, P98 adds cross-fitted selection-valid certification, P99 aggregates valid cross-fitted evidence with e-values, and P100 adds the sequential layer across fresh certification rounds.

What Research II has actually established: a 100-result mathematical and computational framework for stating a sufficiency claim precisely, checking whether its target and measurement assumptions are defensible, rejecting declared model families when the evidence truly separates from them, and keeping that rejection statistically valid through adaptive and sequential analysis. Taken together, all 100 propositions define and audit that testing pipeline. The 100 results form several dependency branches. What it has not established: that consciousness is nonphysical, that a rejected descriptor has no richer physical replacement, or that a model which survives the tests is therefore the true theory of consciousness.

Research II · Current theorem frontier · P100

The current endpoint makes repeated fresh testing valid even when the stopping time is adaptive.

In plain language, P100 answers a practical statistical problem at the end of the Research II pipeline: after one carefully protected test round is valid, can we run another fresh round, inspect the accumulated evidence after every round, and stop when the evidence becomes decisive without invalidating the error guarantee? Under its declared freshness and predictability assumptions, P100 proves that we can.

Fresh round

Collect certification data that were not used to choose their own test

Past results may guide the next plan, but the current certification outcomes cannot leak into that plan.

Accumulate

Turn each valid round into evidence that can be multiplied safely

A predictable reserve stake controls how much of the accumulated process is invested in the current round.

Stop validly

Inspect after every round without paying a new error penalty for every look

The resulting evidence process has an anytime-valid threshold under the declared sequential null.

Anytime-valid crossing

The product M_t is a nonnegative supermartingale. Ville's inequality controls the probability of ever crossing 1 / alpha.

Exact two-round checkpoint

With E_t = 25 / 2 and eta_t = 1 / 2, each factor is 27 / 4 and M_2 = 729 / 16 = 45.5625 > 20. The declared unique-data totals are 30192 / 30336.

Scientific boundary: current-round planning, calibration, and stake selection must be frozen from past information, and current certification data must remain conditionally valid given that past. P100 does not validate reused-data relabeling, current-round leakage, model acceptance, consciousness identification, nonphysicality, or completion of the physical-to-experiential bridge.

Research III · Consciousness measurement science

What can third-person evidence actually justify about experience?

Research III moves from bridge logic to measurement science. It separates the experiential target from the evidence used to infer it and asks what reports, behavior, neural measurements, perturbational responses, physiology, interventions, and context can identify, bound, predict, or falsify under explicit assumptions.

Measurement architecture

Experience is the target. Evidence is what science can observe.

Research III keeps five measurement targets separate and requires uncertainty, interpretability, validation domain, assumptions, allowed claims, and forbidden claims to travel with the evidence.

Open the full Research III website guide →

5 separated targets

Presence, global state, content, phenomenal structure, and capacity are treated as distinct scientific questions rather than collapsed into one score.

Two implemented fusion regimes

Point fusion is available when conditional independence is justified; dependence-robust partial identification uses sharp Fréchet-Hoeffding bounds when it is not.

Non-identification is a result

An interval as wide as [0,1] can be scientifically correct when the observations and assumptions do not identify one answer.

Scientific boundary: Research III is a theory-neutral measurement architecture and tested software foundation. It is not a validated universal consciousness meter, and it does not settle the ontology of consciousness.

The project in plain language

The goal is not to declare a consciousness formula. It is to make bridge claims scientifically testable.

The program asks three different questions that are often mixed together: what physical system is being studied, whether a chosen physical description is sufficient for an independent target, and what evidence can justify about experiential claims. Keeping those questions separate makes it possible to expose hidden assumptions, detect non-identification, design discriminating experiments, and say “open” when the evidence does not decide.

Shared scientific boundary

What the combined program has not established

The repository does not claim that consciousness has already been derived from physics. Research I does not turn a recovered physical subsystem into a conscious subject. Research II does not turn conditional model separation into proof of consciousness or nonphysicality. Research III does not turn a measurement scaffold into a universal consciousness meter.

The physical-to-experiential bridge remains an independently testable open problem. The purpose of the three-stage architecture is to narrow that problem without hiding the missing step inside a definition.
