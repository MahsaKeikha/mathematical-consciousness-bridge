About the research program

A mathematical test architecture for one of science's hardest unresolved questions

Mathematical Consciousness Bridge is an ongoing mathematical-physics research program by Mahsa Keikha, PhD. Its purpose is not to assume a definition of consciousness and then fit mathematics around it. The program asks a prior scientific question: what conditions would have to be satisfied before a physical description of a system could legitimately support a testable claim about an independently specified experiential distinction?

The central problem

Physics can describe states, dynamics, interactions, interventions, observables, and measurement probabilities with extraordinary precision. Neuroscience can characterize electrical activity, chemistry, behavior, information flow, responses to stimulation, and many other physical correlates. None of those descriptions, by itself, establishes why a particular physical condition should correspond to a particular experience, whether the chosen physical description contains every distinction that would matter for experience, or whether the target being used to test the claim has been defined independently enough to provide genuine evidence.

The project calls this unresolved connection the physical-to-experiential bridge problem. Rather than hiding that bridge inside a definition, the research decomposes it into scientific obligations that can be proved, measured, falsified, or explicitly left open.

The deterministic form asks whether an independently specified target \(E\) factors through a declared physical descriptor \(T\):

\[E = B\circ T.\]

The stochastic form asks whether the residual target information vanishes once the declared descriptor is known:

\[I(E;\Omega\mid T)=0.\]

These equations are not definitions of consciousness. They are testable sufficiency conditions for a declared descriptor and a declared target under explicit assumptions.

Purpose

Turn a broad philosophical question into scientific obligations

The program separates physical description, target definition, measurement reliability, model adequacy, finite-data uncertainty, and the final bridge claim so that failure at one layer is not hidden by success at another.

Physical side

Demand representation- and scale-defensible structure

A proposed physical descriptor should survive changes of coordinates, interventions, temporal continuation, composition, coarse-graining, and controlled changes of physical scale without silently losing distinctions the claim requires.

Target side

Protect against circularity and measurement erasure

An experiential target must be independently justified rather than manufactured from the same physical descriptor being tested. Its observation channel must also be able to preserve the distinctions the experiment is supposed to detect.

Inference

Separate identification from adequacy

Recovering parameters under a model is not the same as showing that the model is adequate. P73-P83 progressively distinguish identifiability, finite-sample recovery, overidentification, model rejection, and certified full-law separation.

Evidence

Require finite-data and numerical certification

The framework distinguishes population statements from finite experiments and ordinary numerical fits from one-sided certificates strong enough to support a rejection claim.

Falsifiability

Design the theory so it can fail informatively

A descriptor collision, target-circularity diagnosis, unstable target channel, failed adequacy constraint, full-law incompatibility, or uncertifiable optimization step identifies a specific scientific failure rather than being absorbed into an unfalsifiable story.

What this project is not claiming

The program does not claim that consciousness has been derived from physics, that a latent state is automatically an experience, that a neural or information-theoretic quantity is consciousness by definition, that quantum mechanics by itself solves the bridge problem, that consciousness is a new spacetime dimension, or that one failed physical descriptor proves consciousness lies outside physics. The work develops conditional mathematical results and test procedures. Any future physical-to-experiential bridge principle would still require independent formulation, empirical constraints, and opportunities for falsification.

Where this work came from

From physical subsystem identification to the bridge problem

This research continues the earlier Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics project, which asks how a persistent moving subsystem can be identified from measured stochastic dynamics. That earlier problem is physical: how to recover a time-dependent system boundary from data. Mathematical Consciousness Bridge begins after that kind of physical-description problem and asks what additional obligations arise before a physical description can support a claim about experience.

The distinction matters. Identifying a coherent subsystem, a causal organization, an information pattern, or even a complete physical state does not by itself identify subjective experience. The present program therefore keeps the physical modeling problem and the experiential bridge problem logically separate.

Scientific boundary

The repository does not claim that consciousness has already been derived from physics. A proved theorem is a theorem under its declared assumptions. An implementation is not empirical evidence. A latent state is not automatically experience. A non-rejected model is not automatically valid. And a complete quantum description is still a physical description unless an independently justified bridge principle connects it to experiential structure.

Research architecture

One scientific chain, with different failure modes isolated instead of hidden

The 83 results form several dependency branches. Proposition number records development order; the scientific structure is better understood by function.

01

Physical system

Declare states, dynamics, interventions, observables, system boundary, and the physical information actually being tested.

02

Operational structure

Require invariance, causal meaning, temporal continuation, composition, and controlled scale changes.

03

Physical sufficiency

P19-P24 ask whether a declared physical descriptor screens off an independently specified target and whether that conclusion survives finite/adaptive data.

04

Target integrity

P71-P74 ask whether the target is non-circular, whether its measurement can erase evidence, and whether target-channel reliability is identifiable and finite-sample certifiable.

05

Model adequacy

P75-P83 test the target-measurement model itself, culminating in exact-rational global continuous-family separation certificates.

06

Finite-data decision

Separate certified rejection from inconclusive non-rejection using the correct lower-bound and upper-bound directions.

07

Bridge claim

The physical-to-experiential step remains open and must still be independently formulated, constrained, and exposed to falsification.

Current target-side frontier

P71-P83: from target provenance to certified full-law separation

This branch returns directly to the P19 sufficiency question and asks whether the target, its observation process, its latent measurement model, and the global finite-data rejection logic are all defensible before any experiential interpretation is entertained.

P71

Target provenance

Descriptor-derived targets can make factorization succeed by construction. Independent provenance is a scientific requirement, not something inferred from a zero residual alone.

P72

Measurement robustness

Under the declared nondifferential channel model, noisy target measurement can attenuate or erase a real witness; a null observation is not automatically a null latent distinction.

P73

Channel identifiability

Three nondegenerate conditionally independent binary views can identify the declared latent measurement channel up to a global label swap; two are insufficient in general.

P74

Finite-data recovery

Simultaneous confidence bounds are propagated through the P73 inversion, with an explicit gate that refuses unstable recovery near the covariance singularity.

P75

Model adequacy

Parameter recovery is separated from model validation. A fourth view creates overidentifying restrictions and a full sixteen-cell reconstruction audit.

P76

Finite adequacy rejection

An apparent population violation must remain separated from zero after finite-sample uncertainty is propagated before rejection is certified.

P77

Full-law separation

The complete empirical confidence region is compared with the complete declared model set. A local best fit is explicitly not a global rejection certificate.

P78

Certified continuous separation

Exact-rational branch-and-bound supplies a global lower bound on distance to the continuous P75 family and an explicit mesh-gap certificate.

P79

Certified sampling radius

Exact-rational logarithm and square-root enclosures give the statistical uncertainty side the correct one-sided numerical direction.

P80

Simplex-coupled tightening

Probability normalization is retained inside every parameter-box relaxation, producing a lower bound that is never weaker than P78.

P81

Projection-event tightening

Exact parameter-box ranges for all nonempty projected binary events add marginal and cylinder-event constraints that can strictly strengthen P80.

Falsification

The framework is designed to fail informatively

Target circularity

Do not treat a descriptor-derived target as independent evidence that the same descriptor is sufficient.

Measurement failure

Do not infer a latent null result when the declared target channel can erase a witness.

Identifiability failure

Do not report channel reliability when the measurement model is degenerate or observationally underidentified.

Model-adequacy failure

Reject the declared target-measurement model when independent overidentifying or full-law constraints fail.

Optimization failure

Do not convert a candidate numerical fit into a global separation claim without a certified lower bound.

Descriptor collision

A pair with the same declared physical descriptor but independently distinguishable target structure falsifies sufficiency of that descriptor.

What success would actually look like

Not one impressive equation labeled "consciousness." A scientifically serious result would require a clearly specified physical system, a representation- and scale-defensible physical description, an independently justified experiential target, a trustworthy measurement model, a bridge rule with explicit assumptions, finite-data uncertainty control, and experiments capable of proving the rule wrong if it is false.
