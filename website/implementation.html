Implementation guide

How the research actually works

This page connects the conceptual research map to the repository that executes and audits it. Each stage below shows the scientific question, the computational transformation, representative source modules, and the proof/test/reproducibility surfaces a reader can inspect directly.

Important scientific boundary: executable mathematics is not evidence that the physical-to-experiential bridge has been solved. The code implements declared mathematical objects, tests, bounds, optimization procedures, and falsification criteria under stated assumptions. The bridge target remains open.

Stage 01 · P1-P10

Foundations: make a bridge claim mathematically testable

The first layer turns broad claims into objects that can be compared, identified, recovered, and falsified. The implementation represents observational equivalence, identifiable signatures, experimental discrimination, feature sufficiency, and finite-data recovery as explicit functions rather than prose-only concepts.

InputCandidate states, descriptors, observations, tolerances, or finite samples.
OperationConstruct equivalence classes/signatures, compare distinguishability, and quantify recovery or sample requirements.
OutputAn identifiable object, a recovery result, a finite-data certificate, or an explicit failure condition.

Stage 02 · P11-P18

Structured physical candidates: causal, temporal, compositional, and scale-aware structure

This stage asks what information a proposed physical descriptor actually retains. Code constructs intervention-resolved causal structure, temporal structure, compositions, and controlled coarse-grainings so later bridge tests operate on declared physical objects rather than an undefined notion of “the brain” or “the physical state.”

InputInterventions, responses, delays, components, partitions, and aggregation maps.
OperationBuild causal/temporal structure, compose subsystems, then quantify what changes or survives under coarse-graining.
OutputA structured physical descriptor with explicit invariance and information-loss boundaries.

Stage 03 · P19-P24

Bridge sufficiency tests: does the target factor through the declared physics?

The central operational test is not “does physics correlate with the target?” but whether the declared target is determined by, or statistically factors through, the declared physical descriptor under the chosen bridge class. Residuals expose distinctions the descriptor fails to capture; finite-sample and repeated-look modules decide when that failure is statistically certifiable.

InputA physical descriptor X, an independently declared target Y, a bridge class, and observed samples.
OperationTest exact or approximate factorization, compute residual witnesses, refine descriptors, and control adaptive/repeated inference.
OutputA sufficiency certificate, a nonfactorization witness, or an inconclusive finite-data result.
fundamental_physical_sufficiency.pyCore P19-style factorization interface finite_sample_residual_certification.pyFinite-data residual certificate descriptor_refinement_residual.pyResidual behavior under descriptor refinement adaptive_descriptor_selection.pyValidity under data-dependent descriptor choice anytime_refinement_certification.pyRepeated-look / anytime certification

Interpretation: failure of one descriptor does not prove that experience is nonphysical. It proves only that the declared descriptor/bridge class is insufficient for the independently declared target under the tested conditions.

Stage 04 · P71

Target provenance: prevent a bridge from succeeding by construction

P71 audits where the target came from. If the target is itself constructed from the same descriptor being tested, factorization can become circular. The implementation records target-construction dependencies and tests whether the alleged evidence is independent of the physical descriptor whose sufficiency is under examination.

InputDescriptor, target, and target-construction protocol.
OperationTrace provenance and detect target definitions that encode the tested factorization.
OutputA non-circularity status or an explicit circularity warning.
P71 proof & assumptionsFormal proposition and scientific boundary target_provenance_noncircularity.pyExecutable provenance audit

Stage 05 · P72

Target measurement: model what the observation channel can hide

The target and the measured report are not treated as identical. P72 inserts an explicit observation channel between them and quantifies how noise can attenuate or erase a real target-side distinction. This prevents “no measured difference” from being silently equated with “no latent difference.”

InputLatent target distinction plus a declared noisy measurement channel.
OperationPropagate the target distribution through the channel and compute retained/attenuated separation.
OutputObserved witness strength and channel-stability bounds.
P72 proof & assumptionsFormal noisy-channel robustness statement target_measurement_channel_robustness.pyExecutable channel propagation and bounds

Stage 06 · P73-P84

Channel recovery and model adequacy: learn the measurement model, then try to falsify it

P73-P84 build a continuous chain from channel recovery to certified model-family separation. P73-P76 separate four questions that are easy to conflate: population identifiability, finite-sample recovery, independent model adequacy, and finite-sample rejection. Three nondegenerate conditionally independent binary views can identify the declared latent channel up to label swap; a fourth view creates overidentifying constraints that can expose model failure.

InputThree- or four-view binary observations, a declared latent conditional-independence model, and the parameter boxes used for certified full-law separation.
OperationRecover channel parameters, test model adequacy, certify full-law distance, and progressively retain simplex, projection, nested-event, parity, and joint shared-parameter constraints.
OutputCertified recovery, model rejection, or an explicitly inconclusive result.

Frontier continuation: P77-P84 strengthen this branch from individual adequacy constraints to certified separation from the complete continuous model family, including exact rational parameter-box, simplex, projected-event, nested-residual, parity, and joint parity-contrast certificates. P84 preserves shared-parameter compatibility across pairs of P83 parity events and has an exact strict witness with L83 = 0 and L84 = 1/32. See the P84 frontier.

Stage 07 · P25-P37

Operational scale: test whether structure survives changes of resolution

A bridge claim should not depend unknowingly on an arbitrary microscopic labeling. These modules aggregate nodes/states, construct operational quotients, and test compatibility of intervention, influence, irreducibility, response geometry, and delay information across controlled changes of scale.

InputFine-scale physical structure plus a declared aggregation or quotient map.
OperationPush operational quantities through the map and compare fine/coarse representations.
OutputCompatibility, quantified loss, or a scale-dependent failure witness.

Stage 08 · P38-P44

Quantum interface: separate operational quantum completeness from experiential completeness

The quantum branch treats density operators, measurement statistics, tomography uncertainty, and regular bridge classes as physical/operational objects. It deliberately does not infer that a complete quantum description is already a theory of experience. The executable routines ask what can be certified from finite quantum data and what remains dependent on an explicit bridge assumption.

InputQuantum states/regions, measurement data, tomography error, and a declared regular bridge class.
OperationTest operational sufficiency, propagate trace-distance uncertainty, construct envelopes, and derive finite-sample requirements.
OutputQuantum-operational certificates and explicit limits on what they imply about the bridge.
quantum_operational_sufficiency.pyOperational quantum sufficiency continuous_quantum_region_regularity.pyRegularity over continuous quantum regions finite_data_quantum_nonfactorization.pyFinite-data quantum nonfactorization tests trace_ball_quantum_envelope.pyTrace-ball uncertainty envelope quantum_regular_bridge_sample_complexity.pySample-complexity bounds

Stage 09 · P45-P53

Adaptive evidence acquisition: decide where the next measurement should go

Once multiple candidate witnesses share experimental resources, the problem becomes sequential. These modules allocate samples across witness graphs, protect validity under adaptive selection/stopping, and update residual demand as evidence accumulates.

InputCandidate witnesses, uncertainty/gaps, shared preparation resources, budgets, and current evidence.
OperationAllocate measurements, update confidence states, schedule service, and re-optimize remaining demand.
OutputA valid next-sample policy, stopping status, or resource/service guarantee.

Stage 10 · P54-P70

Execution and calibration: make the experimental schedule itself certifiable

The final stage makes switching, transition measurement, metric uncertainty, and calibration costs explicit optimization variables. Continuous relaxations, exact integer allocation, heterogeneous costs, and primal-dual diagnostics are implemented separately so computational convenience does not silently replace a certificate.

InputTransition/switching costs, calibration uncertainty, integer measurement counts, lower bounds, and heterogeneous resource costs.
OperationOptimize routes/allocations, propagate metric uncertainty, solve exact integer calibration, and compute primal-dual/global optimality diagnostics.
OutputAn executable allocation/schedule plus a certificate or quantified optimality gap.

Reading rule

Use this page to understand the implementation path, then open the proposition proof for the precise assumptions and claim. Code demonstrates that a declared mathematical procedure has been implemented; tests check that the implementation behaves as specified; neither one upgrades a conditional theorem into an empirical fact about consciousness.
