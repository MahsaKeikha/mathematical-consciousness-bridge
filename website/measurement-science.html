Research III · Consciousness Measurement Science

From observable evidence to claims that can be identified, stress-tested, reproduced, and refused when the evidence is insufficient.

Research III is the measurement-science stage of the program. It does not define consciousness by a convenient biomarker. It asks what a declared experiential target can actually support when report, behavior, neural signals, perturbational responses, physiology, interventions, context, calibration uncertainty, dependence, missingness, transport, and site heterogeneity are made explicit.

Central measurement model

Separate the latent target from the observable channel

For the formal validation program, let \(Z\in\{0,1\}\) be a declared latent measurement target and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) an observable binary evidence channel. Let \(\pi=P(Z=1)\), sensitivity \(\alpha=P(Y=1\mid Z=1)\), specificity \(\beta=P(Y=0\mid Z=0)\), and \(q=P(Y=1)\).

\[q=(1-\beta)+(\alpha+\beta-1)\pi.\]

\[J=\alpha+\beta-1,\qquad \boxed{\pi=\frac{q+\beta-1}{J}}\quad\text{when }J>0.\]

The equation is not the conclusion. V1-V25 tests identification, finite-sample uncertainty, transport, dependence, structural falsification, finite calibration error, missingness, inverse conditioning, multisite identifiability, sharp identified sets, sample-size design, and release rules that separate pilot selection from confirmatory inference.

Electromagnetic measurement arm

EM fields are measurable evidence. Whether they carry consciousness-specific information is an empirical question.

Research III now includes a dedicated electromagnetic program using EEG, MEG, OPM-MEG, perturbational recordings, or other field measurements as candidate observables. The current result is not “electromagnetism equals consciousness.” The result is a falsifiable measurement program with explicit nuisance controls and failure tests.

V16-V20 · electromagnetic observables

Equal power does not imply equal field organization

V18 matches channel power to numerical precision while spatial phase concentration changes from 1 to approximately 0. V19 shows a shared contaminant can create apparent global organization. V20 shows organization can differ sharply across frequencies in the same multichannel signal.

Electromagnetic source-identifiability arm

A sensor pattern and a reconstructed source are not the same scientific object.

V21-V25 formalize the EEG/MEG-style forward model \(\mathbf y=\mathbf L\mathbf j+\boldsymbol\eta\) and test what source conclusions survive reference choice, lead-field ambiguity, inverse regularization, modality combination, and forward-model perturbation.

V21-V25 · forward and inverse limits

Perfect sensor fit does not imply a unique source

The canonical V22 lead field has rank 3 and nullity 3: two source vectors separated by norm 1 produce exactly the same sensor measurement. V23 shows inverse-prior sensitivity, V24 shows complementary modalities can reduce but not erase ambiguity, and V25 bounds forward-model mismatch.

Scientific boundary

What V1-V25 establishes, and what it does not

Established mathematically

Under declared binary-channel assumptions, the inverse, partial-identification bounds, finite-sample guarantees, transport-bias equation, conditioning behavior, two-site identified sets, and V10 marginal erroneous-release bound are explicit and testable.

Established computationally

Fixed-seed simulations exercise coverage, dependence failure, structural null/power behavior, calibration uncertainty, missingness, site heterogeneity, release rate, and empirical conditional coverage after abstention.

Not human validation

No current V1-V25 analytic or synthetic validation shows that a neural, behavioral, physiological, perturbational, electromagnetic sensor, reconstructed source, or artificial-system signal has the assumed calibration for consciousness.

No universal biomarker

No scalar, EEG/MEG measure, reconstructed source, imaging feature, complexity metric, or behavioral channel is declared to equal consciousness.

No ontology by inversion

Recovering an abstract latent target under a calibrated measurement model does not establish what consciousness is metaphysically.

No forced answer

Partial identification and abstention are valid scientific outputs when the evidence cannot support the requested resolution.

Next empirical layer

The next scientific burden is prospective calibration and external validation

The mathematical and software machinery is now explicit enough to be challenged with real data. Stronger empirical claims will require target-specific calibration, preregistered endpoints, participant and site holdout, altered-state transport, confound controls, causal perturbation where relevant, repeated multimodal assessment, and independent replication.
