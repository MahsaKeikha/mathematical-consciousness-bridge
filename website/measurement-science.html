Research III · Consciousness Measurement Science

From observable evidence to claims that can be identified, stress-tested, reproduced, and refused when the evidence is insufficient.

Research III is the measurement-science stage of the program. It does not define consciousness by a convenient biomarker. It asks what a declared experiential target can actually support when report, behavior, neural signals, perturbational responses, physiology, interventions, context, calibration uncertainty, dependence, missingness, transport, and site heterogeneity are made explicit.

Central measurement model

Separate the latent target from the observable channel

For the formal validation program, let \(Z\in\{0,1\}\) be a declared latent measurement target and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) an observable binary evidence channel. Let \(\pi=P(Z=1)\), sensitivity \(\alpha=P(Y=1\mid Z=1)\), specificity \(\beta=P(Y=0\mid Z=0)\), and \(q=P(Y=1)\).

\[q=(1-\beta)+(\alpha+\beta-1)\pi.\]

\[J=\alpha+\beta-1,\qquad \boxed{\pi=\frac{q+\beta-1}{J}}\quad\text{when }J>0.\]

The equation is not the conclusion. V1-V20 tests identification, finite-sample uncertainty, transport, dependence, structural falsification, finite calibration error, missingness, inverse conditioning, multisite identifiability, sharp identified sets, sample-size design, and release rules that separate pilot selection from confirmatory inference.

Electromagnetic measurement · V16-V20

Measure electromagnetic organization without mistaking the observable for consciousness

Research III now includes a dedicated electromagnetic measurement arm. EEG, MEG, OPM-MEG, and related recordings can provide physically measurable evidence about neural field dynamics, but no field amplitude, power, coherence, entropy, rank, or phase statistic is treated as consciousness itself. The present V16-V20 record validates the measurement mathematics and its failure modes before any consciousness-specific empirical claim is allowed.

\[\mathcal F(\mathbf r,t)=\{\mathbf E(\mathbf r,t),\mathbf B(\mathbf r,t)\},\qquad \mathbf X(t)=\mathcal M(\mathcal F)(t)+\boldsymbol\eta(t).\]

\[\Phi_{EM}=(H_f,C_\phi,r_{\mathrm{eff}},H_{SV},\rho_{CM}).\]

V16

Physical field sanity layer

Implements electromagnetic energy density and Poynting flux as unit-checked physical calculations. These equations are sanity checks, not a direct model of field propagation through biological tissue.

V17

Gain-invariant organization

Normalized spectral, phase, covariance-rank, and singular-value descriptors remain unchanged under the declared global gain sweep of 0.1, 1, and 10.

V18

Power is not enough

Two eight-sensor 10 Hz fields have matched channel power to relative error below 3 x 10^-16 while phase concentration changes from 1 to numerical zero. Power alone cannot identify spatial field organization.

V19

Common-mode confound

A shared contaminant raises apparent phase concentration from numerical zero to about 0.935 in the synthetic stress test. Shared environmental, reference, and field-spread effects therefore require explicit controls.

V20

Frequency-specific structure

The same multichannel construction has phase concentration 1 at 10 Hz and numerical zero at 17 Hz. Field organization must be declared in frequency, space, time, and acquisition context.

Scientific boundary: V16-V20 currently support M0 physical and measurement-design claims only. They establish that electromagnetic measurements can carry structured, testable information and that simple power or synchrony summaries can be non-identifying or confounded. They do not establish an electromagnetic consciousness signature, direct access to qualia, a universal field threshold, or an electromagnetic ontology of consciousness.

Scientific boundary

What V1-V20 establishes, and what it does not

Established mathematically

Under declared binary-channel assumptions, the inverse, partial-identification bounds, finite-sample guarantees, transport-bias equation, conditioning behavior, two-site identified sets, and V10 marginal erroneous-release bound are explicit and testable.

Established computationally

Fixed-seed simulations exercise coverage, dependence failure, structural null/power behavior, calibration uncertainty, missingness, site heterogeneity, release rate, and empirical conditional coverage after abstention. Deterministic V16-V20 constructions additionally test electromagnetic gain invariance, matched-power non-identifiability, common-mode contamination, and frequency-specific field organization.

Not human validation

No current V1-V20 analytic or synthetic validation shows that a neural, behavioral, physiological, perturbational, or artificial-system signal has the assumed calibration for consciousness.

No universal biomarker

No scalar, EEG measure, imaging feature, complexity metric, or behavioral channel is declared to equal consciousness.

No ontology by inversion

Recovering an abstract latent target under a calibrated measurement model does not establish what consciousness is metaphysically.

No forced answer

Partial identification and abstention are valid scientific outputs when the evidence cannot support the requested resolution.

Next empirical layer

The next scientific burden is prospective calibration and external validation

The mathematical and software machinery is now explicit enough to be challenged with real data. Stronger empirical claims will require target-specific calibration, preregistered endpoints, participant and site holdout, altered-state transport, confound controls, causal perturbation where relevant, repeated multimodal assessment, and independent replication.
