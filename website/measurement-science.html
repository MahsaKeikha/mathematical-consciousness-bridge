Research III · Consciousness Measurement Science

What would it take to measure consciousness without defining it by the signal we happen to record?

This stage begins after the physical observer and bridge-test problems have been separated. It asks what reports, behavior, neural measurements, perturbational responses, physiology, interventions, and context can scientifically justify about an explicitly declared experiential target, with uncertainty and non-identifiability kept visible.

The handoff from Research II

Bridge mathematics constrains what a claim must mean. Research III asks how evidence can support that claim in real experiments.

Research II keeps the physical-to-experiential bridge scientifically accountable through explicit descriptors, target provenance, identifiability, finite-data uncertainty, model adequacy, and falsification. Research III does not treat those results as a consciousness meter. It uses them as methodological constraints while building a separate measurement program.

Central measurement question

Experience is the target. Evidence is what science can observe.

Let \(E\) denote an experiential target. Let \(R\) be report, \(B\) behavior, \(N\) neural measurements, \(P\) perturbational responses, \(A\) autonomic and ocular physiology, \(I\) interventions, and \(C\) context.

\[D=(R,B,N,P,A,I,C).\]

\[\boxed{\text{Given }D\text{ and assumptions }\mathcal A,\;\text{what can be identified, bounded, predicted, or falsified about }E?}\]

Identification rule: when the data and assumptions do not determine a unique experiential conclusion, the allowed output is an identification region, competing explanations, or an inconclusive result. The program does not manufacture a point estimate merely because one is convenient.

Five measurement targets

Do not collapse distinct scientific questions into one number

1

Presence

What evidence supports that any experience is occurring under the declared scope?

2

Global state

What multidimensional condition characterizes arousal, connectedness, complexity, stability, and cognitive availability?

3

Content

What is being experienced, and how well can that content be predicted or decoded under validated conditions?

4

Phenomenal structure

What relational organization holds among reported qualities, similarities, discriminations, and experiential dimensions?

5

Capacity

Does the system have the causal or dynamical organization associated with supporting experience even when content cannot be accessed?

Research arm A

State and content inference

The first arm builds a target-specific evidence profile rather than declaring one signal to be consciousness. It records channel values, data quality, uncertainty, assumptions, validation domain, and the strongest claim the evidence is allowed to support.

\[\mathbf V=(V_R,V_B,V_N,V_P,V_A).\]

A scalar score is permitted only after prospective calibration, held-out validation, cross-state testing, confound controls, abstention behavior, and evidence that combining channels adds value over the best single channel.

Negative evidence rule

An uninterpretable or negative channel does not by itself support absence of experience. A negative result can count against the target only when the sensitivity and failure behavior of that channel are separately validated for the relevant context.

Research arm B

Phenomenal structure measurement

For communicative participants, Research III can construct a phenomenal relational space from similarity judgments, discrimination, ordering, and structured reports, then compare it with a physical or neural relational space built from population activity, effective connectivity, perturbational response, or representational geometry.

\[D(f)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal P|}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal P}\left|\tilde d_E(e_i,e_j)-\tilde d_N(n_i,n_j)\right|.\]

Low distortion supports a declared structural-correspondence hypothesis. High distortion rejects that mapping family. Neither result by itself proves that neural structure is experience.

Candidate mathematical tools

Representational similarity, multidimensional scaling, optimal transport, Gromov-Wasserstein comparison, persistent homology, category theory, and local-to-global sheaf constructions can be used only when their empirical role and testable predictions are explicit.

Claim ladder

Strong claims require stronger validation

M0 to M2

Condition differences, out-of-sample prediction, and generalization across participants or sites within a declared domain.

M3 to M5

Cross-state generalization, confound and causal tests, then multimodal identification or bounds under explicit assumptions.

M6 to M7

Held-out phenomenal-to-physical structural prediction and unique preregistered theory predictions that outperform rivals.

Boundary: no level in this ladder, by itself, licenses the statement that qualia have been directly measured. A stronger ontological conclusion would require an independently defensible bridge principle and evidence that distinguishes it from alternatives.

Research program

From benchmarks to adversarial theory tests

Phase 1 benchmark

Healthy waking, near-threshold perception, report and no-report conditions, sleep, altered states, and multimodal quality control.

Cross-state program

Sleep, multiple anesthesia mechanisms, dissociative states, confound dissection, and causal perturbation.

Disorders of consciousness

Serial behavioral assessment, task EEG or fMRI where feasible, resting measures, perturbational evidence, and explicit uncertainty.

Adversarial theory tournament

Precommit theory-specific predictions, rival predictions, controls, failure rules, and auxiliary assumptions before observing outcomes.

Edge cases

Separate evidential standards for infants, animals, organoids, and AI rather than copying adult human thresholds across substrates.

Statistical validation

Calibration, measurement invariance, transportability, partial identification, dependence sensitivity, and failure-domain publication.

Scientific boundary

Research III is a measurement program, not an ontological shortcut

No ontology by assumption

Physicalist, emergentist, dual-aspect, fundamental, and other interpretations remain open unless evidence distinguishes them.

No universal biomarker by declaration

EEG complexity, PCI, connectivity, report, or behavior must be validated for a declared target and domain.

No response is not no experience

Motor failure, task failure, or an insensitive neural test can erase evidence without erasing experience.

No quantum shortcut

A quantum-specific account must make discriminating predictions that outperform relevant classical alternatives.

No hidden dependence assumption

Multimodal fusion must model or stress-test channel dependence rather than silently multiplying evidence as if channels were independent.

No clinical overreach

The repository is not a clinical device and does not replace specialist evaluation or justify life-support decisions.

The complete progression

Research I defines the physical observer. Research II disciplines the bridge. Research III builds the measurement science.

The three stages are intentionally distinct. That separation is what allows the program to advance without turning an operational observer, a mathematical theorem, or an attractive biomarker into a conclusion about consciousness that the evidence has not earned.
