Research I: physical subsystem identification

Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics

Before asking whether a physical description can support a claim about experience, we first need to know which physical system is being described. Research I asks whether a persistent subsystem can be recovered from measured dynamics when its boundary changes through time.

The question

Can the boundary of a physical subsystem be inferred instead of assumed?

Many systems do not come with a permanently fixed boundary. Components move, exchange information, appear, disappear, or become more or less tightly coupled.

Research I asks whether the observed dynamics support one moving subsystem path more strongly than competing paths.

Why this matters here

A consciousness bridge should not begin with an ambiguous physical object. Research I helps make the physical side explicit before Research II asks what that physical description can support.

The physical intuition

Four ideas guide the moving boundary search

The earlier project uses these ideas to decide whether a candidate subsystem behaves like a coherent physical unit through time.

Integration

Do the internal parts matter to one another?

A candidate subsystem should show meaningful internal dependence rather than behaving like an arbitrary collection of coordinates.

Insulation

How much extra information comes from outside?

A coherent subsystem should not require the entire exterior state to predict its next state once its own state is known.

Persistence

Does the organization remain coherent through time?

The subsystem should preserve meaningful structure instead of changing arbitrarily from one moment to the next.

Transport

Can the organization continue while the coordinates carrying it change?

This allows a persistent physical pattern to move through the measured system instead of being tied to one fixed set of coordinates.

The physical inference pipeline

Measured observables support a statistical description of the dynamics. Candidate subsystem boundaries are then scored and compared across time. The final output is a recovered physical path under explicit assumptions and uncertainty.
