The Research I question

Can a moving subsystem boundary be inferred from the dynamics rather than assumed in advance?

Let the measured state at time \(t\) be \(X_t=(X_t^{(1)},\ldots,X_t^{(n)})\). A candidate subsystem is a time-dependent subset \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Research I asks whether the measured dynamics distinguish one moving sequence of subsets from competing paths after identifiability and finite-data uncertainty are taken into account.

Research I formulation

A self-contained moving-subsystem identification problem

Research I starts from an operational question: when the measured coordinates carrying a coherent subsystem can change through time, can the dynamics identify a preferred moving boundary rather than requiring that boundary to be fixed in advance?

Problem definition

Infer the boundary from measured dynamics

The candidate object is a time-indexed sequence of subsets. Integration, insulation, persistence, and transport are treated as declared dynamical ingredients, then combined with global path optimization and finite-sample certification.

Scientific boundary

Physical subsystem identification comes first

The framework addresses whether a moving physical subsystem can be recovered from data under explicit assumptions. It does not identify subjective experience, establish consciousness, or close the physical-to-experiential bridge.

What is developed in Research I

The moving world-tube objective, transport functional, recovery theory, identifiability analysis, physical-time calibration, finite-sample certification, and reproducibility record are developed as part of this research program and are evaluated through their own assumptions, derivations, implementations, tests, and numerical experiments.

Physical observation model

The mathematics begins from an explicit effective stochastic dynamical model

The main analytical observation model in Research I is

\[X_{t+1}=A_tX_t+\varepsilon_t,\qquad \varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,Q_t),\qquad \varepsilon_t\perp X_t.\]

What the model provides

With \(\Sigma_t=\operatorname{Cov}(X_t)\), the model determines the adjacent-state fluctuation geometry used to compute Gaussian information and canonical-correlation quantities. Research I treats this as an effective statistical model, not as a universal microscopic physical law.

The interpretation of coordinates, units, sampling schedule, nuisance structure, covariance geometry, and candidate boundaries must therefore be declared and validated for each physical application.

The inferred object

The observer is represented operationally as a moving world-tube

At each time, the candidate boundary is \(S_t\). Across a finite horizon, the moving subsystem is the path

\[\mathcal W=(S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_{T-1}).\]

The word observer is operational in Research I: it denotes the candidate subsystem supported by the declared dynamical criterion. Recovering a world-tube is a physical subsystem-identification result, not a conclusion about subjective experience.

Global recovery objective

The preferred observer path is a finite-horizon optimization problem

The local score combines integration, insulation, and persistence; the path objective then rewards transport and penalizes excessive boundary discontinuity.

\[\Omega_t(S)=\left[G_t(S)K_t(S)P(X_S^t,X_S^{t+1})\right]^{1/3}.\]

\[A(p)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Omega_t(S^{(j_t)})+\chi\sum_{t=0}^{T-2}\Theta_t(S^{(j_t)}\to S^{(j_{t+1})})-\lambda\sum_{t=0}^{T-2}d_J(S^{(j_t)},S^{(j_{t+1})}).\]

Why the path formulation matters

The problem is global: a plausible boundary at one time is not enough. Dynamic programming selects a complete trajectory through the candidate family and also provides the runner-up needed to define the population action margin \(\Delta_A=A(p_*)-A(p_{(2)})\).

That separation margin becomes central when finite-sample uncertainty is propagated back to path recovery.

Scientific boundary

What Research I established, and what it deliberately did not establish

Research I develops a mathematical and computational framework for time-dependent physical subsystem identification. It studies moving-boundary recovery, identifiability, finite-sample uncertainty, temporal dependence, nuisance structure, physical-time calibration, innovation whitening, and propagation of covariance uncertainty back to the world-tube objective. It does not prove that the recovered subsystem is conscious, that dynamical persistence is subjective identity, or that a successful physical observer model solves the physical-to-experiential bridge.

What Research I now hands to Research II

Not just one winning boundary: a traceable inference object

The downstream program needs to know how the physical subsystem was obtained. The handoff therefore records the selected world-tube, competing paths and margins, finite-data assumptions, measurement geometry, and whether the boundary was frozen on data independent of downstream certification.

This prevents a hidden change of status from estimated subsystem to known subsystem. When uncertainty remains material, the next stage must retain multiple admissible world-tubes and test robustness across them.
