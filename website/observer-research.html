Physicalobserver-defined operational architecture

The question of Research I

How can an observer-centered physical description be made mathematically explicit without defining consciousness into the model?

The first repository develops a precise language for moving observers, physical subsystems, causal accessibility, multimodal measurements, comparison metrics, interventions, and uncertainty. Its purpose is operational: specify what a physical observer can access, measure, compare, perturb, and infer over time.

The core observer object

The program organizes an observer through a structured object

\[O=(W,\Sigma(\tau),\Pi,F,M,C),\]

where \(W\) is the observer world-tube, \(\Sigma(\tau)\) is its evolving physical state or history, \(\Pi\) is a subsystem partition, \(F\) is a family of observable extractors, \(M\) is a metric family, and \(C\) records event and causal structure.

This makes the observer an auditable mathematical object rather than a verbal placeholder. Each component can be changed, tested, or falsified independently.

Scientific boundary

Research I does not insert consciousness as a scalar, state variable, field, material phase, or extra spacetime dimension. It studies operational invariants and observer-defined physical structure.

Agreement between operational metrics is evidence of robustness of the physical description. It is not ontological proof about subjective experience.

From spacetime events to the information physically available to an observer

Causal accessibility

For an observer worldline \(\gamma_O(\tau)\), an event or physical occurrence \(X\) is accessible when it lies on or within the observer's past light cone:

\[A_O(\tau;X)=\mathbf 1\!\left[X\text{ occurs before or on the past light cone at }\gamma_O(\tau)\right].\]

This separates what exists in the modeled physical system from what can physically enter the observer's information history at time \(\tau\).

Instantaneous and finite-horizon readouts

A multimodal instantaneous physical readout can be written as

\[X_O(\tau)=\sum_k w_k\,\phi_k(\tau),\]

while a finite-horizon observer history is

\[\mathcal X_O[\tau_a,\tau_b]=\{X_O(\tau)\mid \tau\in[\tau_a,\tau_b]\}.\]

The finite-horizon form matters because observer identity and physical organization are generally dynamical rather than single-frame objects.

Comparison geometry

Observers can be compared without pretending one metric is an ontology

Research I combines observable, causal, and decision-level discrepancies into a declared metric family rather than hiding modeling choices inside a single unexplained score.

\[S_{\theta}(O_i,O_j)=\alpha d_{\mathrm{obs}}+\beta d_{\mathrm{causal}}+\gamma d_{\mathrm{dec}},\]

with a normalized default form

\[S_{\theta}=\frac{\alpha d_{\mathrm{obs}}+\beta d_{\mathrm{causal}}+\gamma d_{\mathrm{dec}}}{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}.\]

The parameters make the comparison rule visible. Sensitivity to those choices can therefore be measured rather than ignored.

Mathematical results

What Research I established inside its declared physical model

The original repository records theorem-like and computational results across spacetime consistency, causal accessibility, observer comparison, covariance, and perturbational analysis.

01

Exact worldline consistency

Constant-velocity worldline calculations agree with the declared relativistic geometry and provide a controlled reference case.

02

Accessibility monotonicity

The causal-accessibility construction has a mathematically controlled ordering as the observer's physically available history grows.

03

Well-posed observer score

The normalized composite score is defined under explicit nonnegative weights and exposes the dependence on the chosen metric family.

04

Zero-distance verification

Matched observer descriptions return the expected zero-separation behavior under the implemented metric definitions.

05

Positive mismatch separation

Physically or operationally mismatched observer descriptions can be separated by the declared comparison geometry.

06

Lorentz-covariant event classification

The causal classification of events is checked against the relevant Lorentz transformations rather than tied to one arbitrary inertial coordinate frame.

07

Directional causal-drift proxy

Perturbational changes are tracked directionally to distinguish simple descriptive difference from intervention-sensitive causal drift.

Inference and validation

The physical architecture was built with separate deterministic, statistical, intervention, and robustness layers

01

Deterministic geometry

Worldlines, world-tubes, Lorentz transformations, proper time, null-cone accessibility, and exact reference cases.

02

Observation layer

Finite-horizon multimodal readouts and explicit estimators distinguish the physical state from the data available to inference.

03

Intervention profile

Perturbations test whether apparent similarity persists when the modeled system is actively changed rather than passively observed.

04

Reliability and uncertainty

Noise, estimation uncertainty, parameter dependence, and representation robustness are treated as scientific quantities rather than presentation details.

05

Adversarial counterexamples

Counterexamples expose conditions under which an intuitively attractive observer score or structural interpretation can fail.

06

Preregistration

Claims, expected signatures, and failure conditions are separated from post-hoc interpretation wherever the program makes empirical-facing proposals.

What Research I did not establish

It did not prove that an observer object is conscious, that operational persistence is subjective identity, that causal integration is experience, or that a robust physical metric is a consciousness measure. The program supplies a disciplined physical and observer-indexed description. The physical-to-experiential step is a different scientific obligation.

Why Research II exists

A strong physical description is the starting point of the bridge question, not its answer

Research I can provide an observer-indexed physical descriptor, schematically \(\Sigma_O\). The present repository then asks a distinct question: under what independently justified assumptions could a target variable \(C\) depend only on that physical description?

\[\Sigma_O \quad\longrightarrow\quad ? \quad\longrightarrow\quad C\]

\[C=G(\Sigma_O)\quad\text{is a bridge hypothesis, not a consequence of defining }\Sigma_O.\]
