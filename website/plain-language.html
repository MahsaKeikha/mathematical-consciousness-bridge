The whole project in plain English

One difficult question, separated into three scientific stages

People can describe and report subjective experience from the first-person point of view. Science usually works with measurements that can be observed, recorded, compared, and tested from the outside. The central question is: how can we build a rigorous scientific bridge between those two kinds of statement?

The project does not assume that experience is outside physics. It also does not assume that a physical measurement automatically explains experience. Instead, it breaks the problem into stages so that each assumption can be inspected and tested.

Research I

First, identify the physical system

Before asking what a system experiences, we need to know what physical system we are even talking about. This stage studies how a persistent subsystem or observer-like physical unit can be identified from changing dynamics and measurements.

Simple question: what is the physical thing that remains meaningfully the same system while its state and surroundings change?

Open Research I →

Research II

Then, test the proposed bridge

Once a physical system and a physical description are declared, this stage asks whether that description is actually sufficient for an independently specified experience-related target.

Simple test: if two cases look the same according to the proposed physical description but the independently defined target distinguishes them, then the declared description is missing something needed for that target.

This is the 92-result Research II theorem program currently reaching P92.

Explore Research II →

Research III

Finally, make the evidence measurable

Even a beautiful theorem is not enough if the experience-related evidence is noisy, circular, dependent, or poorly measured. This stage develops measurement science for combining reports, behavior, neural activity, physiological signals, interventions, context, and uncertainty.

Important principle: when the evidence does not identify a unique answer, the scientifically correct result may be a range, several competing explanations, or simply “we do not yet know.”

The project is not trying to force a predetermined answer

The larger goal is to build scientific machinery that can compare competing ideas, expose missing assumptions, show when evidence is insufficient, and make proposed physical-to-experiential claims falsifiable. The final question remains open: what relationship, if any, connects physical reality to subjective experience?

The 60-second version

The project starts with a simple gap

Science can measure a great deal about a physical system. In a brain, for example, we can study electrical activity, chemistry, blood flow, behavior, information flow, responses to stimulation, and interactions between regions. Physics can describe states and dynamics at even more fundamental levels.

But describing what a system is physically doing is not automatically the same as explaining what, if anything, is being experienced. To connect those two statements scientifically, we need more than a correlation and more than an impressive equation.

This project asks what a proposed connection would have to survive before we should trust it: missing physical information, changes of representation, changes of scale, circular definitions, unreliable measurements, inadequate statistical models, limited data, and numerical uncertainty.

One sentence

We are building mathematical and measurement-science tests for whether a proposed physical description is actually sufficient for an independently defined experience-related claim.

If the test fails, we want to know exactly what failed. If it passes, we still ask what has and has not been established.

A simple analogy

Think of a physical description as a map

A map can be excellent for one purpose and incomplete for another. A subway map may be perfect for finding a train route, but useless for telling you the height of a building. The map is not wrong. It simply does not contain that information.

A physical model can have the same problem. It may predict behavior, signals, or measurements extremely well and still leave out a distinction required by a particular claim about experience.

So one of our basic tests is this: if two situations look identical according to the physical description we chose, can an independently justified target still distinguish them? If yes, that physical description is not sufficient for that target.

What that would mean

It would show that the declared description is missing something needed for the claim.

It would not prove that consciousness is outside physics. The model may be too coarse, use the wrong variables, use the wrong system boundary, or omit relevant physical information.

The research questions in ordinary language

Seven questions have to be kept separate

Much of the repository exists because these questions are often mixed together. We keep them separate so that a success at one stage cannot hide a failure at another.

01

What system are we talking about?

We must define the physical system, its boundary, what we can measure, how it changes over time, and what interventions are allowed.

02

What physical information are we using?

We must state exactly which physical features are supposed to matter and check that the conclusion is not an accident of coordinates, scale, or representation.

03

What experience-related distinction are we testing?

The target cannot simply be created from the same physical variables and then presented as independent evidence that those variables explain it.

04

Can our measurement of that target be trusted?

Reports, behavior, clinical labels, and other observations can be noisy or incomplete. A measurement process can weaken or erase a real distinction.

05

Does the measurement model itself fit reality?

Even if a model can estimate hidden variables, that does not mean the model is correct. We need independent checks that can reject the model when it fails.

06

Are the data strong enough?

Finite experiments contain noise. The mathematics must separate a genuine incompatibility from an apparent difference that could easily arise by chance.

07

What would finally connect physics to experience?

Only after the earlier layers are defensible does the final bridge question become meaningful. That bridge remains open and must be independently formulated and tested.

Why this is different from choosing a consciousness formula

We do not start by declaring that one quantity is consciousness

The project does not assume that consciousness is a particular neural pattern, information score, complexity measure, field, quantum effect, latent variable, or new dimension. Any of those ideas could be proposed as part of a theory, but the proposal still has to survive the same scientific obligations.

The goal is to build a framework in which competing ideas can be challenged on common ground: What exactly is the physical description? What target is it supposed to explain? How was that target defined? How was it measured? What would prove the proposed description insufficient? What uncertainty remains?

Where are we now?

Some foundations are built. The central scientific bridge is not.

The project is deliberately explicit about the difference between completed mathematical work and questions that remain open.

Built

Physical-system identification foundations

Research I develops mathematics for identifying a persistent physical subsystem from changing measured dynamics. This helps define the physical object of study before any claim about experience is made.

Built

A 92-result sufficiency and falsification architecture

Research II develops exact and finite-data tests for representation, scale, target provenance, model adequacy, and physical-description sufficiency, currently through P93.

Built

A dependence-aware measurement-science foundation

Research III now includes an implemented uncertainty-aware framework that can combine evidence under explicit assumptions and can return partial identification when dependence prevents a unique answer.

Not established

No universal consciousness biomarker

The project has not shown that one brain signal, information quantity, field, latent state, or other physical variable is a universal measure of consciousness.

Not established

No proof of nonphysicality

Failure of a declared physical descriptor means that descriptor is insufficient for the declared target. It does not prove that no richer physical account could succeed.

Still open

The physical-to-experiential bridge

The final bridge still requires independently justified empirical, causal, cross-state, multimodal, and theory-discriminating tests.

How to read a result

Pass, fail, and inconclusive mean different things

If a descriptor fails

We have learned that this description is insufficient

We have not proved that no physical description could work. The next scientific question is what information, scale, boundary, or assumption was missing.

If a model is rejected

We have learned that the declared model cannot explain the data under its assumptions

That is useful because it narrows the space of acceptable explanations without turning the rejection into a metaphysical conclusion.

If a test does not reject

The correct conclusion may simply be inconclusive

The model may be compatible with the data, or the experiment may be too weak to distinguish it from alternatives. Non-rejection is not proof.

If everything so far passes

The final bridge still has to be justified

Good physical modeling, good measurement, and good statistics create the conditions for a serious bridge test. They do not by themselves produce the bridge.

What the whole research program is doing

Three stages answer three different scientific questions

The proposition sequence belongs to Research II. It is one stage of a larger program, not the entire project. Research I identifies the physical system; Research II tests whether a declared physical description is sufficient and falsifiable; Research III asks what the available evidence can actually identify about an experiential target.

How to interpret progress

A stronger Research II theorem does not replace Research I or Research III. The stages constrain different failure modes, and the final physical-to-experiential bridge remains open until the system definition, bridge test, and measurement evidence all support the same claim.

What this project does not claim

It does not claim that consciousness has already been derived from physics. It does not claim that a latent statistical state is automatically an experience. It does not claim that quantum mechanics by itself solves the problem. It does not claim that failure of one physical descriptor proves consciousness is nonphysical. And it does not treat a successful fit as proof of a complete bridge.

P92 is the current checkpoint, not the destination

The goal is not simply to keep adding proposition numbers

P92 is the current mathematical checkpoint inside a much larger research program. The purpose of the theorem sequence is to make assumptions behind consciousness research increasingly explicit, testable, and falsifiable.

The destination is not Proposition 91, 100, or 200. The destination is a scientific framework capable of telling us, with increasing confidence, what our physical descriptions and measurements really allow us to say about experience, what they do not allow us to say, and which experiment could distinguish competing explanations.

The nonlinear separation survives arbitrary latent mixing

P90 used one extreme prevalence face. P91 allows both latent components. A rank-two flattening constraint gives a global lower exclusion greater than 1/42, while an explicit mixed model point gives an upper bound of 1/24. The exact global optimum inside that bracket remains open.

Research II · Historical exact single-component frontier · P90

Exact nonlinear rank-one slice separation

P90 moves beyond the complete P89 linear parity-functional envelope by exploiting a nonlinear identity of the declared strict P75 model image. With prevalence fixed at zero, the active law is one product Bernoulli component, so the canonical two-by-two slice must satisfy ad = bc. The empirical determinant residual is 5/192, giving the exact distance L90 = 5/72 = (7/3)L89, while P89 remains the complete-linear subfrontier at 5/168.

This is a conditional exact separation result for the stated strict box. It does not identify consciousness, establish nonphysicality, exhaust more general nonlinear mixture regimes, or close the physical-to-experiential bridge.
