Research lineage

First identify the physical system. Then ask what that physical description can support.

The consciousness research grew from an earlier physical inference problem. The first project asks whether a persistent subsystem can be identified from measured dynamics when its boundary changes through time. The second project asks what additional evidence would be needed before a description of that subsystem could support a scientific claim about experience.

The distinction that matters

The two projects are connected, but they solve different problems

Recovering a coherent physical subsystem can strengthen the physical side of the research. It does not identify subjective experience. The bridge question begins only after the physical system has been described clearly.

Research I: physical subsystem identification

Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics

The first repository studies a moving physical boundary. Instead of assuming in advance which measured coordinates belong to the subsystem, it asks whether the dynamics support one coherent path more strongly than alternatives.

The research examines internal integration, environmental insulation, persistence through time, transport through changing coordinates, identifiability, uncertainty, and reproducibility.

The word observer is used operationally. A recovered subsystem is not automatically a conscious subject.

The scientific handoff

What carries forward?

Research II: the bridge test program

Mathematical Consciousness Bridge

The second repository begins after the physical system has been declared. It asks whether a chosen physical description is sufficient for an independently motivated target, whether that target can be measured reliably, whether the model itself can be challenged, and whether any apparent result survives uncertainty.

The mathematics becomes more detailed as the questions become more specific, but the logic remains simple: define the claim clearly enough that evidence can count against it.
