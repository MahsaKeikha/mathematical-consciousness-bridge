Research lineage

From physical observer identification to bridge tests to consciousness measurement

This research program now has three explicit stages. Research I formalizes the physical observer subsystem. Research II asks what a physical-to-experiential bridge claim must survive before it deserves scientific weight. Research III asks how experiential targets can be measured through multimodal evidence, causal intervention, calibration, structural correspondence, and explicit uncertainty without pretending that third-person signals are qualia themselves.

The transition in one sentence

Research I asks which physical subsystem persists? Research II asks what makes a bridge claim scientifically defensible? Research III asks what can actually be measured about experience, and under which assumptions?

The stages are connected, but they solve different problems. Read this page from Research I to Research II to Research III. A coherent physical subsystem does not by itself establish experience. A bridge theorem does not by itself produce a validated consciousness measure. Each stage therefore carries forward only what the previous stage actually established and introduces a new set of falsifiable obligations.

Research I · physical subsystem identification

Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics

The first repository develops a mathematical and computational framework for identifying a time-dependent subsystem boundary from stochastic dynamics. The candidate object is a moving world-tube rather than a static partition.

01

Recover a moving physical boundary from measured dynamics

At time \(t\), a candidate subsystem is \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and the moving history is a world-tube

\[\mathcal W=(S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_{T-1}).\]

The project scores candidate paths using declared dynamical properties such as internal integration, environmental insulation, predictive persistence, and transport through changing measured coordinates. Recovery is treated as a global path problem with explicit identifiability and finite-sample uncertainty.

What Research I establishes, and what it deliberately does not

Research I addresses physical subsystem identification: whether measured dynamics support one moving subsystem path more strongly than alternatives under a declared statistical model and uncertainty budget. Its use of the word observer is operational. A recovered subsystem is not automatically a conscious subject, an experiential state, or a solution to the physical-to-experiential bridge.

↓

The unresolved step

A well-defined physical subsystem still leaves the bridge question open

Bridge remains an independently testable open problem. Identifying a coherent physical subsystem is necessary for a precise physical description, but it does not by itself establish any claim about subjective experience.

Once a candidate subsystem and its dynamics are physically specified, the next scientific question is not “therefore, where is consciousness?” It is whether the declared physical description contains the distinctions required by an independently specified experiential target, whether the target itself is non-circular and measurable, and whether finite data can actually discriminate model adequacy from model failure.

Research II · bridge test architecture

Mathematical Consciousness Bridge

The second repository begins where the first physical-description problem leaves off. It asks what a physical-to-experiential claim must survive before it deserves scientific weight.

02

Test sufficiency, target integrity, model adequacy, and finite-data evidence

The central deterministic question asks whether an independently specified target \(E\) factors through a declared physical descriptor \(T\):

\[E=B\circ T.\]

The stochastic version asks whether target-relevant residual information vanishes after conditioning on the descriptor:

\[I(E;\Omega\mid T)=0.\]

These are sufficiency tests, not definitions of consciousness. The program adds representation invariance, scale compatibility, target provenance, target-channel reliability, finite-sample uncertainty, model-adequacy rejection, exact certification, and explicit falsification boundaries.

From selection-valid evidence to anytime-valid sequential accumulation

This is the current endpoint of the Research II theorem sequence. It belongs before the handoff to Research III because it concerns validity of evidence accumulation for the declared bridge-testing program.

P98 established rotated independent-block certification, P99 converted valid cross-fitted evidence into exact e-values, and P100 adds a fresh-round sequential layer with predictable reserve stakes. The physical-to-experiential bridge remains open.

The next unresolved step

A falsifiable bridge architecture still does not tell us how to measure consciousness in a real subject

Research II disciplines the logic of a bridge claim. Research III turns to measurement science: which experiential target is being inferred, what evidence channels are informative, how confounds and failed channels are handled, what causal interventions discriminate rival mechanisms, and when the data support only a bound or an inconclusive result rather than a point estimate.

Research III · consciousness measurement science

Consciousness Measurement Science: from target definition to cross-site replication

The third repository is a multimodal, causal, and structural measurement-science program that makes its scientific commitments explicit rather than claiming theory neutrality. Its executable record now spans V1-V50: target definition and identification, calibration and transport, electromagnetic observables, source non-identifiability, inverse resolution, spatial specificity, finite-sample inference, multiplicity control, post-selection failure analysis, independent confirmation, and cross-site replication stability. Target choice, evidence channels, validation domains, and claim ceilings are treated as assumptions to expose and test.

03

Infer experiential targets from evidence without collapsing experience into a biomarker

Let E denote an experiential target and let D = (R, B, N, P, A, I, C) collect report, behavior, neural measurements, perturbational responses, autonomic and ocular physiology, interventions, and context. The core question is:

\[\text{Given }D\text{ and assumptions }\mathcal A,\;\text{what can be identified, bounded, predicted, or falsified about }E?\]

The program separates presence, global state, content, phenomenal structure, and capacity. It also separates necessary, sufficient, enabling, and merely correlated conditions. The V1-V50 validation record now makes calibration, inverse ambiguity, spatial resolution, finite-sample covariance estimation, search multiplicity, same-data selection bias, independent confirmation, site heterogeneity, leave-one-site-out influence, partial-conjunction replicability, and evidential weight concentration explicit. When data do not identify the target, the allowed output remains an identification region, competing explanations, or an inconclusive result.

How Research III evolved

Eight linked validation layers make the measurement chain explicit from target definition to cross-site replication inference

The sequence is cumulative. Later source-space and statistical claims inherit the acquisition, identification, calibration, resolution, and design assumptions established earlier. A result at one layer does not bypass the scientific burden at another.

V1-V15 · identification and validation

Finite-sample identification, calibration uncertainty, missingness, dependence, transport, multisite heterogeneity, resolution-aware sample size, and independent release gates.

V16-V20 · electromagnetic observables

Field energy, Poynting flow, gain-invariant organization, equal-power counterexamples, common-mode confounding, and frequency-specific structure.

V21-V25 · source identifiability

Reference invariance, lead-field null spaces, regularization sensitivity, multimodal complementarity, and forward-model perturbation.

V26-V30 · resolution and information limits

Noise whitening, inverse-resolution leakage, a rank-only resolution floor, Fisher information, exact temporal aliasing, and worst-case inverse noise amplification.

V31-V35 · design and spatial specificity

Point-spread and cross-talk functions, covariance-aware source distinguishability, Fisher-information sensor design, nuisance-subspace information loss, and robust model uncertainty.

V36-V40 · finite-sample electromagnetic inference

Efficient GLS amplitude inference, inverse-covariance bias, exact Gaussian source-discrimination error, search-wide error control, and covariance-mismatch calibration.

V41-V45 · multiplicity and selection-safe inference

Arbitrary-dependence family-wise error control, Holm step-down testing, exact sign-flip inference, post-selection coverage failure, and independent holdout confirmation.

V46-V50 · cross-site replication inference and stability

Inverse-variance pooling, common-effect heterogeneity, leave-one-site-out influence, partial-conjunction replicability, and site-weight concentration.
V40

Finite-sample inference makes covariance estimation and search-wide error part of the measurement model

V36-V40 move beyond nominal information limits to the behavior of estimators with finite data. The program derives efficient generalized least-squares amplitude inference, quantifies inverse-covariance bias, connects Gaussian source separation to exact classification error, makes search-wide false-positive inflation explicit, and uses sandwich variance to expose covariance-mismatch miscalibration.

The methodological consequence is direct: a clean source-space estimate is not enough. The covariance estimator, sample size, comparison count, and uncertainty model determine whether the reported precision is actually calibrated.

V45

The current Research III frontier makes search and selection part of the scientific claim

V41-V45 close a major statistical loophole in high-dimensional electromagnetic analysis. Bonferroni controls family-wise error under arbitrary dependence, Holm improves step-down efficiency, exact sign-flip inference provides a finite randomization test under symmetry, same-data selection is shown to destroy ordinary marginal coverage, and independent holdout confirmation restores selected-coordinate Type I error under the declared null.

This strengthens the measurement pipeline without changing the claim ceiling: statistically valid source inference is still not direct evidence that a source is consciousness or qualia.

What Research III does not assume

Research III does not assume that consciousness is nonphysical, that a neural signal is consciousness, that complexity alone proves experience, that statistical significance identifies an experiential target, or that one scalar can serve as a universal consciousness meter. Its output is always conditional on a declared target, validation domain, evidence quality, multiplicity and selection discipline, and explicit assumptions.

Scientific continuity without overclaiming

The three repositories form a research progression, not a proof chain to consciousness

Research I strengthens the physical side of the problem. Research II makes bridge claims mathematically accountable. Research III develops the measurement science needed to connect real evidence to explicitly declared experiential targets. None of those handoffs licenses an ontological conclusion that the preceding stage did not establish.
