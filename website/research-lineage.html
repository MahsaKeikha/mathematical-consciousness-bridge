Mathematical Consciousness Bridge

Research lineage

From identifying a physical observer to testing what a physical description can support

The present consciousness research did not begin by assigning consciousness to a variable. It grew out of an earlier physical inference problem: whether a persistent, moving subsystem can be recovered from measured dynamics without fixing its boundary in advance. Once that physical subsystem problem is made explicit, a harder question becomes unavoidable: what additional evidence would be required before any physical description of that subsystem could support a scientifically testable claim about experience?

The transition in one sentence

Research I asks which physical subsystem persists? Research II asks what would make a claim from that physics to experience scientifically defensible?

The projects are connected, but they solve different problems. Recovering a coherent physical subsystem is a prerequisite for many observer-centered physical questions. It is not, by itself, a derivation of subjective experience. The second program begins by preserving that distinction.

Research I · physical subsystem identification

Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics

The first repository develops a mathematical and computational framework for identifying a time-dependent subsystem boundary from stochastic dynamics. The candidate object is a moving world-tube rather than a static partition.

01

Recover a moving physical boundary from measured dynamics

At time \(t\), a candidate subsystem is \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and the moving history is a world-tube

\[\mathcal W=(S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_{T-1}).\]

The project scores candidate paths using declared dynamical properties such as internal integration, environmental insulation, predictive persistence, and transport through changing measured coordinates. Recovery is treated as a global path problem with explicit identifiability and finite-sample uncertainty.

What Research I establishes, and what it deliberately does not

Research I addresses physical subsystem identification: whether measured dynamics support one moving subsystem path more strongly than alternatives under a declared statistical model and uncertainty budget. Its use of the word observer is operational. A recovered subsystem is not automatically a conscious subject, an experiential state, or a solution to the physical-to-experiential bridge.

↓

The unresolved step

A well-defined physical subsystem still leaves the bridge question open

Once a candidate subsystem and its dynamics are physically specified, the next scientific question is not “therefore, where is consciousness?” It is whether the declared physical description contains the distinctions required by an independently specified experiential target, whether the target itself is non-circular and measurable, and whether finite data can actually discriminate model adequacy from model failure.

Research II · bridge test architecture

Mathematical Consciousness Bridge

The second repository begins where the first physical-description problem leaves off. It asks what a physical-to-experiential claim must survive before it deserves scientific weight.

02

Test sufficiency, target integrity, model adequacy, and finite-data evidence

The central deterministic question asks whether an independently specified target \(E\) factors through a declared physical descriptor \(T\):

\[E=B\circ T.\]

The stochastic version asks whether target-relevant residual information vanishes after conditioning on the descriptor:

\[I(E;\Omega\mid T)=0.\]

These are sufficiency tests, not definitions of consciousness. The program adds representation invariance, scale compatibility, target provenance, target-channel reliability, finite-sample uncertainty, model-adequacy rejection, exact certification, and explicit falsification boundaries.

Scientific continuity without overclaiming

The two repositories form a research progression, not a proof chain to consciousness

The earlier project provides a disciplined physical-subsystem inference framework. The present project uses that kind of explicit physical modeling as methodological groundwork, then introduces additional obligations that are specific to a physical-to-experiential claim. Success in Research I therefore strengthens the quality of the physical side of the problem; it does not eliminate the need for an independently justified experiential target or bridge principle.
