How to use this page

Each figure answers one question

Read the short explanation first, then inspect the figure. If it makes you curious, follow the link to its formal context. The figures explain structure and results, but they do not replace proofs or empirical evidence.

1. The whole program

Where does the bridge problem sit?

The research begins with physical description, moves through measurable structure and sufficiency tests, then asks whether the target and model are scientifically usable. The final connection to experience remains open.

Research architecture

Follow the figure as a dependency map. The arrows show which questions must be addressed before stronger claims become meaningful. They do not say that one layer has already been identified with consciousness.

2. The handoff from physical system to bridge question

What does the earlier observer mathematics contribute?

The earlier project identifies a physical subsystem. This repository begins after that physical object is declared and asks what additional conditions are needed before an experience related claim can be tested.

Observer to bridge handoff

The key point is separation. Identifying the physical system does not insert an experiential property into it. The bridge question begins afterward.

3. The measurement problem

Different observations are not automatically the same evidence

Behavior, report, perturbational response, and other measurements may carry different information. A bridge theory has to say what each observation is supposed to measure.

Measurement and dissociation map

The figure separates evidence channels instead of treating them as interchangeable. That separation becomes important when a target is noisy, incomplete, or difficult to observe directly.

Explore the target and measurement branch

4. The proof ladder

What would a strong bridge claim have to survive?

A compelling equation is not enough. The physical side, target side, experimental interface, finite data reasoning, and falsification logic each add a separate obligation.

Universal proof ladder

The ladder is a requirements map. It shows why a bridge claim can fail at several scientifically different levels before the final experiential question is reached.

5. The quantum boundary

Operational completeness is not automatically experiential completeness

A complete quantum description for a declared set of physical predictions still does not, by itself, specify the missing bridge to experience.

Quantum bridge completeness map

The visual keeps quantum state reconstruction on the physical side and the experiential bridge as a separate scientific requirement.
