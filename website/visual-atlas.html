Visual research atlas

Figures as navigational aids to the mathematics

The figures below are selected entry points into the formal record. They are diagrams, quantitative illustrations, or computational visualizations. They do not replace proofs, and each should be read together with its theorem, assumptions, and provenance.

How to read every figure

What you are seeing: identify the mathematical objects, panels, axes, or regions. How to read it: follow arrows only as the declared logical, temporal, set-inclusion, or computational relation; compare plotted quantities using the labeled axes and legends. Main takeaway: use the accompanying text to identify the precise conclusion the visual supports. Scientific boundary: diagrams, synthetic examples, and simulations do not become empirical consciousness evidence merely because they are visually compelling.

Figures are intentionally capped at a readable page size: large enough to inspect, but not so large that the explanatory text disappears below the fold. Complex theorem figures use a wider presentation than simple atlas thumbnails, and the full-resolution SVG remains available from the figure link.

For a direct index of every SVG, including figures not selected for this web page, open the Complete Figure Catalog. The repository-wide rules are in the Figure Caption and Description Standard and the Reader Experience and Visual Presentation Standard.
