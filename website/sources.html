Research origins

An intellectual starting point and a continuing line of questions

The earliest conceptual line that eventually led to this research program began while studying Max Tegmark's Consciousness as a State of Matter. Its treatment of information, integration, independence, dynamics, subsystem structure, and factorization provided an important conceptual starting point for questions that this project later pursued in a further mathematical direction.

Reference: Max Tegmark, “Consciousness as a State of Matter,” Chaos, Solitons & Fractals 76 (2015), 238-270. DOI 10.1016/j.chaos.2015.03.014 · arXiv:1401.1219.

Role of this reference: Tegmark's paper is cited as an important conceptual starting point for the research program. The subsequent proposition sequence develops a distinct mathematical framework whose results are supported by their own assumptions, derivations, implementations, tests, and reproducibility record.

Claim classes

Six evidence roles

Every consequential statement should have an identifiable evidential role. The role determines what kind of citation or audit trail is required.

A

Standard mathematics

Established mathematical results are cited to appropriate scholarly sources when nontrivial and are not relabeled as original results.

B

Established physical theory

Quantum and classical formalisms are sourced and their domains and assumptions remain explicit.

C

External empirical evidence

Measured neural, behavioral, or physiological evidence is distinguished from mathematical proof and from experiential interpretation.

D

Repository-original result

Local propositions are identified by number and tied to proof, code, tests, figures, provenance, and dependencies.

E

Declared assumption or construction

Model classes, bridge classes, regularity conditions, synthetic witnesses, and generated examples are labeled as assumptions or constructions rather than external facts.

F

Open target

Hypotheses, conjectures, and unresolved bridge questions are visibly labeled as open and are not formatted as established results.

Audit path

How to verify a proposition independently

1

Read assumptions

Identify spaces, distributions, interventions, regularity conditions, and finite-data assumptions before reading the conclusion.

2

Check dependencies

Follow cited standard results and earlier propositions through the equation/citation map and theorem roadmap.

3

Inspect proof

Verify that each implication is justified under the stated assumptions and that boundary cases are not hidden.

4

Run implementation

When the theorem is computational, compare the implementation to the mathematical definition and complexity statement.

5

Run tests

Check regression, counterexample, and edge-case behavior across the supported environment matrix.

6

Check scientific boundary

Confirm that the interpretation does not claim more than the theorem or evidence establishes.

Citation principle

The strongest presentation is the one that makes its dependencies easiest to inspect. External claims are sourced externally. Repository-original claims are linked to their local proof record. Assumptions and synthetic constructions are labeled. Open questions remain open until a theorem or experiment changes their status.
