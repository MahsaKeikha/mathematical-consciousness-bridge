Sources and reproducibility

Every important claim should have a visible path back to its source

This page is the audit entrance. It helps you tell whether a statement comes from established mathematics, established physics, external evidence, a repository theorem, a declared assumption, or an open research question.

Evidence roles

Not every statement is supported in the same way

The repository keeps these roles separate so mathematical proof, empirical evidence, assumptions, and open questions are not blurred together.

A

Standard mathematics

Established mathematical results are treated as established results and sourced when appropriate.

B

Established physics

Classical and quantum formalisms are used within their stated domains and are not presented as new discoveries.

C

External evidence

Empirical observations are kept distinct from mathematical proof and from experiential interpretation.

D

Repository results

Original proposition level results are linked to assumptions, proof, code, tests, figures, and provenance.

E

Declared assumptions

Model classes, synthetic witnesses, regularity conditions, and constructions are labeled as assumptions or constructions.

F

Open questions

Unresolved bridge questions remain visibly open until a theorem or experiment changes their status.

Research origin

One important conceptual starting point

An early line of questions behind this program developed while studying Max Tegmark's Consciousness as a State of Matter, particularly its treatment of information, integration, independence, dynamics, subsystem structure, and factorization.

The repository developed those questions in a distinct mathematical direction with its own assumptions, propositions, implementations, tests, and reproducibility record.

Max Tegmark, Consciousness as a State of Matter, Chaos, Solitons & Fractals 76 (2015), 238 to 270.

Audit path

How to verify a proposition without getting lost

Use the same six step path for any mature result.

1

Question

Identify the scientific problem the proposition is trying to solve.

2

Assumptions

Read what must be true before the theorem applies.

3

Proof

Check the mathematical implication under those assumptions.

4

Implementation

Compare the code with the mathematical object when computation is involved.

5

Tests

Check regression, edge case, and counterexample behavior.

6

Boundary

Confirm that the interpretation does not claim more than the result establishes.

Reproducibility

The technical record is designed to be rerun

Proof documents live under docs/, executable mathematics under src/consciousness_bridge/, regression tests under tests/, and source controlled figures under docs/figures/.

The reproducibility guide explains the environment, commands, checks, and publication verification used by the repository.

Source principle

The strongest claim is the easiest claim to audit

External facts should point to external sources. Repository results should point to their local proof record. Assumptions should be labeled as assumptions. Open questions should remain open.
