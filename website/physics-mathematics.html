Physics and mathematics

The physical formalism is explicit before any experiential interpretation is introduced

This page identifies the mathematical and physical layers used by the research program. Standard results remain standard results, repository-original propositions remain separately labeled, and an operationally complete physical description is not treated as an experiential theory without an additional bridge statement.

Target

Independent target

The target variable used in a bridge test is defined independently of the physical descriptor so that factorization is a substantive claim rather than a tautology.

Core mathematical question

Factorization through a declared physical descriptor

At the abstract level, the research asks whether a target law can be represented as a function or stochastic kernel of the declared physical descriptor. If distinct physical cases collapse to the same descriptor while retaining target-relevant distinctions, the descriptor fails the declared sufficiency test.

\[X \xrightarrow{\;D\;} Z,\qquad Y\overset{?}{\longleftarrow} Z\]

This formal question does not by itself determine what consciousness is. It determines what would have to be true for a specific descriptor to be sufficient for a separately operationalized target.

Mathematical toolchain

The main mathematical families used in the project

Quantum information

Density operators, trace distance, tomography confidence regions, channel contraction, and regularity-based nonfactorization tests.

Quantum boundary

Quantum mechanics is used as a physical description, not as a shortcut to a consciousness claim

The quantum branch asks: if the operational quantum state is declared complete for the physical predictions under study, what mathematical evidence would be needed to show that an independently defined target does not factor through that state under a declared bridge class?

The answer requires a target definition, uncertainty model, and bridge regularity assumptions. It is not obtained merely from superposition, entanglement, measurement, or decoherence.

Scientific reading rule

Every result should be read as a conditional implication under its stated assumptions. A mathematically valid implication can still depend on whether its physical descriptor, experimental intervention, target variable, noise model, and regularity class are scientifically appropriate for a particular application.
