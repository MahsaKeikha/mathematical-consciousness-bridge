# Universal Consciousness Proof Target

## 1. Purpose

This document states the strongest result the Mathematical Consciousness Bridge program is designed to pursue.

A universal consciousness theorem, in the sense used by this project, is not a mathematical quantity that is defined first and then renamed consciousness. It is a **theorem-and-evidence architecture** in which the physical domain, experiential domain, bridge principles, physical signature, observable consequences, and finite-data uncertainty are separately specified and then connected.

The target is

\[
\boxed{
\text{physical first principles}
+
\text{bridge principles}
+
\text{empirically validated discriminants}
+
\text{finite-data certification}
\Longrightarrow
\text{formal experiential property}.
}
\]

The mathematical implication must be proved. The premises connecting physical structure to experience must also be exposed independently to empirical failure.

# 2. Universal-proof criteria

A candidate final theorem is evaluated against the following criteria.

## U1. Physical well-definedness

The physical input must be specified independently of arbitrary coordinates, labels, encodings, and unit conventions.

The theory therefore operates on

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_P
=
\mathcal P/{\sim_P}.
}
\]

The physical object must include a declared state space, dynamics, intervention structure, and observation map at the level needed by the bridge.

Proposition 1 establishes the quotient requirement.

## U2. Experiential well-definedness

The experiential target must be represented by an explicitly declared mathematical space

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_E
=
\mathcal E/{\sim_E}.
}
\]

The relation \(\sim_E\) must state which formal differences correspond to the same experiential structure.

This remains a central open bridge problem and cannot be replaced by a physical score alone.

## U3. Non-definitional bridge

A statement such as

\[
C(p)>c_0
\iff
\text{conscious}(p)
\]

is not sufficient merely because the right-hand side has been defined by the left-hand side.

A bridge principle must have independent physical, phenomenological, logical, or empirical motivation and must generate consequences beyond its own definition.

## U4. Representation invariance

The bridge must descend to the physical quotient:

\[
\boxed{
\bar B:\mathcal Q_P\to\mathcal Q_E.
}
\]

Proposition 1 gives the exact factorization criterion.

## U5. Physical-feature sufficiency

If a physical feature

\[
F:\mathcal Q_P\to\mathcal Z
\]

is proposed as sufficient for the bridge, it must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
F(p)=F(p')
\Longrightarrow
\bar B(p)=\bar B(p').
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\bar B=g\circ F
\]

for a unique map on the realized feature image.

Proposition 5 proves this equivalence and supplies the universal feature-matched counterexample test.

## U6. Bridge completeness

A universal physical signature should approach the stronger condition

\[
\boxed{
F_*(p)=F_*(p')
\iff
\bar B(p)=\bar B(p').
}
\]

on its declared physical domain.

Proposition 6 proves that every bridge has a canonical complete signature

\[
C_B(p)=[p]_{\sim_B}
\]

and that every physically proposed complete signature must be isomorphic to this canonical quotient on its realized image.

The scientific problem is to discover such a signature independently from physical theory and evidence, not to define it from a bridge already assumed.

## U7. Empirical identifiability

A substantive bridge disagreement must be exposed to a declared experiment class \(\Pi\).

For competing theories,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_\Pi(\mathfrak T_1,\mathfrak T_2;q)
=
\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}
\|P_1^{\pi,q}-P_2^{\pi,q}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
}
\]

If

\[
\Delta_\Pi=0,
\]

the theories are observationally non-identifiable within that experimental regime.

Propositions 2 and 3 formalize this statement and its theory-space quotient.

## U8. Cross-theory discrimination and optimal experiment design

The proposed bridge must be compared against serious alternative physical-feature and bridge families, including source-faithful versions of:

- Integrated Information Theory;
- Global Neuronal Workspace Theory;
- Recurrent Processing Theory;
- higher-order approaches;
- predictive-processing, neurorepresentational, and active-inference approaches;
- hybrid and future bridge theories.

The task is mathematical and experimental: identify where the induced probability laws diverge and choose experiments that expose those divergences.

Proposition 4 converts finite complete theory discrimination into a set-cover/maximin experiment-design problem.

## U9. Experimental recoverability of the proposed complete signature

A mathematically complete signature must be recoverable from physically admissible observations and interventions.

For the complete experimental fingerprint

\[
\Psi_\Pi(p)
=
(P^{\pi,p})_{\pi\in\Pi},
\]

Proposition 7 proves that recoverability of \(F_*\) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_\Pi(p)=\Psi_\Pi(p')
\Longrightarrow
F_*(p)=F_*(p').
}
\]

A signature-different pair that is observationally identical under every admissible protocol is an exact no-go counterexample for that experiment class.

## U10. Finite-measurement certification

The physical premises entering the final theorem must be measurable with explicit uncertainty.

For finite signature recovery, Proposition 8 defines

\[
\gamma_S
=
\delta_S-\omega_S,
\]

where \(\delta_S\) is minimum between-signature separation and \(\omega_S\) is maximum within-signature spread.

If the uniform distribution-estimation error is at most \(\varepsilon\), exact recovery is guaranteed when

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_S>4\varepsilon.
}
\]

Thus finite uncertainty is part of the theorem, not an afterthought.

## U11. Explicit sample complexity

For categorical outcome laws with at most \(K\) outcomes, \(N_P\) physical realizations, \(N_\pi\) protocols, and confidence target \(1-\alpha\), Proposition 9 gives the sufficient per-cell trial count

\[
\boxed{
n
\ge
\frac{8K^2}{\gamma_S^2}
\log\left(
\frac{2N_PN_\pi K}{\alpha}
\right).
}
\]

A final bridge theorem must eventually possess analogous finite-data certification for every physical quantity it requires.

The companion Spatiotemporal Observer Mathematics program supplies one candidate machinery for finite-sample certification of moving physical subsystems.

## U12. Temporal, compositional, substrate, and falsification consistency

The theory must state how bridge/signature structure behaves under:

- persistence through time;
- changing physical boundaries;
- splitting and merging;
- independent composition;
- controlled coupling;
- memory disruption;
- altered recurrence or synchronization;
- biological and artificial substrate changes that preserve the proposed essential structure.

At least one central bridge premise must generate a consequence capable of failing under an admissible empirical protocol.

A bridge compatible with every possible observation is not empirically validated by those observations.

# 5. What would make the program distinctive

A mature result should combine all of the following:

1. **Representation theorem:** the bridge and signature are physical rather than coordinate dependent.
2. **Sufficiency/counterexample theorem:** proposed physical signatures can be refuted by feature-matched bridge-different pairs.
3. **Completeness theorem:** the target physical signature has the same equivalence-class geometry as the bridge.
4. **No-go theorem:** experiment classes incapable of resolving bridge distinctions are identified explicitly.
5. **Experiment-design theorem:** protocols are selected to maximize theory/signature discrimination.
6. **Finite-data theorem:** uncertainty and trial counts are sufficient for exact recovery under stated assumptions.
7. **Cross-theory empirical program:** competing contemporary consciousness theories are translated into the same framework and exposed to common discriminating experiments.
8. **Bridge theorem:** the physical signature is connected to a formally justified experiential domain by premises that have survived the preceding tests.

A universal reference should state both what can be inferred and what remains impossible under the declared assumptions.
