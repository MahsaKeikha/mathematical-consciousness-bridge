# Candidate consciousness theory families in a common bridge interface

## 1. Purpose

The Mathematical Consciousness Bridge program is theory-comparative. Before proposing a new physical-to-experiential bridge, it must represent serious existing approaches in a common mathematical language without reducing them to slogans.

This document provides that translation layer.

It is not a claim that every proponent of a named theory endorses one unique mathematical feature. Several theory families contain variants, and some explicitly resist reduction to a single scalar or single computational description.

The common interface is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak T_j
=
(\mathcal F_j,\mathcal B_j,\mathcal M_j,\Pi_j),
}
\]

where:

| Object | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| \(\mathcal F_j\) | physical feature family treated as theoretically relevant |
| \(\mathcal B_j\) | proposed physical-to-experiential bridge architecture |
| \(\mathcal M_j\) | measurement interface producing observable predictions |
| \(\Pi_j\) | intervention and experiment class under which the theory is evaluated |

The point of the interface is not to declare these theories equivalent. It is to make their differences mathematically explicit enough for the identifiability and experiment-design theorems in this repository.

# 2. Integrated Information Theory 4.0

Primary source: Albantakis et al. 2023, IIT 4.0.

IIT 4.0 begins from phenomenal axioms and infers physical postulates. In its operational physical formulation, a substrate is characterized using transition probability functions. Candidate systems are evaluated by intrinsic cause-effect power, system integrated information, maximality, distinctions, and relations.

A source-faithful abstract feature family is therefore not merely a scalar \(\Phi\). Write

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{IIT}}(p)
=
\text{intrinsic cause-effect structure specified by }p
}
\]

with system-level quantities such as \(\phi_s\) entering the selection of maximal substrates or complexes.

The bridge style is constitutive: the unfolded intrinsic cause-effect structure is intended to correspond to the quality and quantity of experience under IIT's postulates.

For this repository, IIT is therefore treated as a high-dimensional structural bridge candidate rather than as the statement "more integration means more consciousness."

**Key source:** Larissa Albantakis et al., "Integrated information theory (IIT) 4.0: Formulating the properties of phenomenal existence in physical terms," *PLOS Computational Biology* 19(10) (2023): e1011465. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465.

# 3. Global Neuronal Workspace Theory

Classical GNW formulations propose that many specialized processors operate unconsciously and that conscious access occurs when information enters a brain-scale workspace through amplification and long-range availability.

A conservative feature-family notation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{GNW}}(p)
=
\text{multilevel workspace organization, ignition, and global availability in }p.
}
\]

This notation deliberately does not reduce GNWT to a software-level broadcast flag. A 2026 clarification by Changeux and Farisco emphasizes that GNW is a multilevel neural model extending from cellular and molecular mechanisms to large-scale network dynamics and should not be conflated with a purely computational Global Workspace Theory.

The 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration directly compared preregistered predictions from GNWT and IIT using fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG. The experiment challenged important predictions of both theories rather than establishing a simple winner.

**Key sources:**

- Stanislas Dehaene and Jean-Pierre Changeux, "Experimental and theoretical approaches to conscious processing," *Neuron* 70(2) (2011): 200-227. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018.
- Jean-Pierre Changeux and Michele Farisco, "The Global Neuronal Workspace as a multilevel model of conscious processing," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 30(6) (2026): 477-479. DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2026.03.004.
- Cogitate Consortium et al., "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness," *Nature* 642 (2025): 133-142. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1.

# 4. Recurrent Processing Theory

Recurrent-processing accounts emphasize recurrent interactions in sensory systems rather than requiring global broadcast as the primary condition for phenomenal consciousness.

A conservative abstract feature family is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{RPT}}(p)
=
\text{recurrent processing structure and dynamics relevant to conscious content in }p.
}
\]

The exact spatial scale, timing, and relation between recurrent processing and report/access must remain theory-version specific.

**Key source:** Victor A. F. Lamme, "Towards a true neural stance on consciousness," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 10(11) (2006): 494-501. DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2006.09.001.

---

# 5. Higher-Order Theory families

Higher-order approaches explain consciousness in terms of a mental state's relation to an appropriate higher-order representation or awareness of that state. The family contains important variants, so the repository does not encode one universal HOT mechanism prematurely.

A generic feature-family notation is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{HOT}}(p)
=
\text{higher-order representational relation instantiated by }p.
}
\]

For experimental comparison, each HOT variant must specify what physical realization counts as the relevant higher-order relation and how it changes predicted observations under intervention.

**Key source:** Richard Brown, Hakwan Lau, and Joseph E. LeDoux, "Understanding the Higher-Order Approach to Consciousness," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 23(9) (2019): 754-768. DOI: 10.1016/j.tics.2019.06.009.

---

# 6. Predictive-processing and neurorepresentational approaches

Predictive processing is not one settled consciousness bridge. Seth and Hohwy explicitly argue that predictive processing may be more useful as a framework for consciousness science than as a monolithic theory identifying consciousness with one mechanism.

The repository therefore represents predictive approaches as a family

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{PP}}(p)
=
\text{hierarchical inferential, predictive, and representational structure in }p,
}
\]

with a theory-specific bridge still required.

Neurorepresentationalism provides a more explicit proposal in which conscious experience is associated with multimodal, situated representations or "superinferences" constructed from body and environment.

A 2026 adversarial collaborative review compares Integrated Information Theory, Neurorepresentationalism, and Active Inference and explicitly frames future progress in terms of divergent predictions, multisite experiments, and quantitative integration of evidence.

**Key sources:**

- Anil K. Seth and Jakob Hohwy, "Predictive processing as an empirical theory for consciousness science," *Cognitive Neuroscience* 12(2) (2021): 89-90. DOI: 10.1080/17588928.2020.1838467.
- Cyriel M. A. Pennartz, "What is neurorepresentationalism? From neural activity and predictive processing to multi-level representations and consciousness," *Behavioural Brain Research* 432 (2022): 113969. DOI: 10.1016/j.bbr.2022.113969.
- Andrew W. Corcoran et al., "Integrated information and predictive processing theories of consciousness: An adversarial collaborative review," *Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews* 187 (2026): 106742. DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2026.106742.

# 7. Cross-theory comparison table

| Theory family | Physical feature family | Bridge style | Important experiment dependence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IIT 4.0 | intrinsic cause-effect structure, maximal substrate, distinctions and relations | phenomenal axioms to physical postulates; constitutive intrinsic structure | perturbational/causal model and state-dependent transition structure |
| GNWT | multilevel workspace organization, ignition, long-range availability | conscious access/content associated with global neuronal workspace dynamics | timing, report, content decoding, long-range and multilevel neural measurements |
| RPT | recurrent processing in relevant sensory/neural circuits | recurrent processing proposed as central for phenomenal consciousness | temporal disruption and local recurrent interactions |
| HOT | higher-order representational relation | a state is conscious in virtue of an appropriate higher-order relation | dissociation of first-order content from higher-order representation/report |
| predictive/NR/active-inference family | hierarchical prediction, inference, multimodal representation, theory-specific generative structure | heterogeneous; no single bridge assumed here | perturbations of prediction, precision, representation, and hierarchical inference |

The table is a translation layer, not a verdict on which theory is correct.

# 8. Why Proposition 5 is needed

Every theory family can be asked the same abstract question.

Suppose a theory proposes a representation-invariant physical feature

\[
F_j:\mathcal Q_P\to\mathcal Z_j.
\]

Under what exact condition can that feature be sufficient to determine the experiential bridge

\[
\bar B:\mathcal Q_P\to\mathcal Q_E?
\]

Proposition 5 answers this without assuming any particular theory:

\[
\boxed{
F_j(p)=F_j(p')
\Longrightarrow
\bar B(p)=\bar B(p')
}
\]

is exactly the condition under which the bridge can factor through the proposed physical feature.

This turns "feature X is sufficient for consciousness" into a precise factorization claim that can be attacked by counterexamples.

# 9. Broader survey context

Seth and Bayne 2022 organize prominent contemporary approaches into higher-order theories, global workspace theories, re-entry and predictive-processing theories, and IIT, and emphasize that it remains unclear how current theories relate or whether they can be empirically distinguished.

A 2025 multi-theory debate report likewise emphasizes substantial disagreement over the explananda, mechanisms, and testability of GNWT, HOT, IIT, RPT, and predictive-processing approaches.

These observations motivate the repository's theory-agnostic quotient, identifiability, and experiment-design framework rather than selection of a preferred theory by literature count.

**Survey source:** Anil K. Seth and Tim Bayne, "Theories of consciousness," *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 23 (2022): 439-452. DOI: 10.1038/s41583-022-00587-4.
