# Proposition 73: three-view target-channel identifiability

**Status:** proved identifiability and finite-sample channel-stability theorem under a declared three-view binary symmetric target-measurement model.

## 1. Purpose

P71 requires a target to be constructed independently enough that a bridge test is not true by definition. P72 then shows how a declared target-measurement channel can attenuate or erase evidence and introduces the channel-stability quantity needed to reason about that attenuation.

P72 intentionally leaves one question open:

> If the latent target is not observed directly, when can the target-measurement channel stability be identified from repeated target observations rather than assumed?

P73 answers that question for a deliberately narrow but exactly solvable model. The result exposes both an identifiability threshold and its limitations.

The theorem does not identify consciousness. It concerns the reliability of repeated measurements of a declared latent binary target under explicit assumptions.

---

## 2. Three-view measurement model

Let

\[
Z=E^\star\in\{-1,+1\}
\]

be the declared latent binary target. Suppose three observed target views satisfy

\[
Y_i=Z N_i,
\qquad i\in\{1,2,3\},
\]

where each

\[
N_i\in\{-1,+1\}
\]

is independent of \(Z\), and \(N_1,N_2,N_3\) are mutually independent.

Define the signed reliability

\[
r_i=\mathbb E[N_i]\in[-1,1]
\]

and the P72 binary channel-stability magnitude

\[
\gamma_i=|r_i|.
\]

If the view is oriented so that its labels agree with the latent labels more often than chance, then \(r_i>0\) and its symmetric error probability is

\[
\eta_i=\frac{1-r_i}{2}.
\]

The observable pairwise sign moments are

\[
m_{ij}=\mathbb E[Y_iY_j].
\]

Because \(Z^2=1\),

\[
Y_iY_j=N_iN_j.
\]

Independence therefore gives

\[
\boxed{m_{ij}=r_i r_j.}
\]

This elementary factorization is the key observable identity used by P73.

---

## 3. Proposition 73A: two heterogeneous views are not individually identifiable

With only two views, the observable constraint is

\[
|m_{12}|=\gamma_1\gamma_2.
\]

For any \(0<|m_{12}|<1\), choose any

\[
\gamma_1\in[|m_{12}|,1].
\]

Then

\[
\gamma_2=\frac{|m_{12}|}{\gamma_1}
\]

is also in \((0,1]\), and every such pair produces the same observable absolute moment.

Hence there is a continuum of compatible heterogeneous channel-stability pairs.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Two heterogeneous repeated views do not identify }(\gamma_1,\gamma_2).
}
\]

An equality assumption such as \(\gamma_1=\gamma_2\) would make the common stability identifiable as \(\sqrt{|m_{12}|}\), but that is an additional structural assumption and is not inferred from two-view data alone.

---

## 4. Proposition 73B: three nondegenerate views identify the stability magnitudes

Assume

\[
m_{12}m_{13}m_{23}>0
\]

and none of the three moments is zero. Under the declared P73 model,

\[
m_{12}=r_1r_2,
\qquad
m_{13}=r_1r_3,
\qquad
m_{23}=r_2r_3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{m_{12}m_{13}}{m_{23}}=r_1^2,
\]

and cyclic permutation gives

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_1
=
\sqrt{\frac{m_{12}m_{13}}{m_{23}}},
\qquad
\gamma_2
=
\sqrt{\frac{m_{12}m_{23}}{m_{13}}},
\qquad
\gamma_3
=
\sqrt{\frac{m_{13}m_{23}}{m_{12}}}.
}
\]

The ratios are positive for any moment triple generated by three nonzero signed reliabilities. A candidate moment triple that produces a squared stability outside \((0,1]\) is incompatible with this binary symmetric three-view model.

Thus the three P72 stability magnitudes are exactly identified from observable pairwise moments.

---

## 5. Proposition 73C: signed reliability has one unavoidable global orientation ambiguity

Suppose two nonzero reliability triples \(r=(r_1,r_2,r_3)\) and \(r'=(r'_1,r'_2,r'_3)\) produce the same three pairwise moments.

By Proposition 73B,

\[
|r_i|=|r'_i|
\]

for each \(i\). Equality of the pairwise products then forces all component signs to change together or not at all. Hence

\[
r'=r
\quad\text{or}\quad
r'=-r.
\]

So the signed reliability vector is identified only up to one global sign flip, while

\[
\boxed{(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)}
\]

is unique.

This distinction matters for the bridge program. P72's witness-stability coefficient for a binary symmetric target channel is

\[
\gamma_i=|1-2\eta_i|=|r_i|,
\]

so the stability magnitude needed to quantify witness preservation is identifiable even when the latent label orientation is not.

If the protocol independently declares the orientation condition

\[
r_i>0
\]

for every view, then

\[
\boxed{
\eta_i=\frac{1-\gamma_i}{2}
}
\]

is uniquely determined.

## 6. Proposition 73D: finite-sample stability intervals

Suppose we observe \(n\) independent triples

\[
(Y_{1k},Y_{2k},Y_{3k}),
\qquad k=1,\ldots,n,
\]

from the declared P73 model. Define

\[
\widehat m_{ij}
=
\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^n Y_{ik}Y_{jk}.
\]

Each product \(Y_{ik}Y_{jk}\) lies in \([-1,1]\). Hoeffding's inequality gives

\[
\Pr\left(
|\widehat m_{ij}-m_{ij}|>\varepsilon
\right)
\le
2\exp\left(-\frac{n\varepsilon^2}{2}\right).
\]

A union bound over the three pairs gives simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) for

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_n(\alpha)
=
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(6/\alpha)}{n}}.
}
\]

Because

\[
\bigl||\widehat m_{ij}|-|m_{ij}|\bigr|
\le
|\widehat m_{ij}-m_{ij}|,
\]

define

\[
L_{ij}=\max\{0,|\widehat m_{ij}|-\varepsilon_n\},
\qquad
U_{ij}=\min\{1,|\widehat m_{ij}|+\varepsilon_n\}.
\]

Then on the simultaneous event,

\[
L_{ij}\le |m_{ij}|\le U_{ij}
\]

for all three pairs.

Since

\[
\gamma_1^2
=
\frac{|m_{12}||m_{13}|}{|m_{23}|},
\]

monotone interval propagation gives

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\frac{L_{12}L_{13}}{U_{23}}}
\le
\gamma_1
\le
\sqrt{\frac{U_{12}U_{13}}{L_{23}}}
}
\]

whenever the relevant denominator bounds are positive, with the obvious trivial bounds \(0\) or \(1\) used when a denominator cannot be separated from zero. Cyclic permutations give the intervals for \(\gamma_2\) and \(\gamma_3\).

Thus P73 turns repeated target views into a finite-data confidence statement about the channel-stability quantities introduced by P72.

A nonzero lower confidence bound on \(\gamma_i\) is a finite-data certificate that the corresponding binary target view is not completely erasing under the declared model.

## 7. Proposition 73E: handoff to P72

P72's prospective finite-sample design depends on a justified lower bound

\[
\gamma_i\ge\gamma_0>0.
\]

P73D supplies a route to such a bound from repeated-view calibration data.

If an independent calibration sample gives

\[
\Pr(\gamma_i\ge L_{\gamma_i})\ge1-\alpha_c,
\]

and the subsequent P72 bridge experiment has its own confidence guarantee at level \(1-\alpha_b\), then a union bound gives joint validity at least

\[
\boxed{1-(\alpha_c+\alpha_b).}
\]

Using the same observations both to estimate the target channel and to certify a bridge witness requires a joint or sample-split analysis. P73 does not silently treat those two uses as independent.

## 8. Exact synthetic audit example

Take

\[
r_1=0.8,
\qquad
r_2=0.6,
\qquad
r_3=0.5.
\]

Then

\[
m_{12}=0.48,
\qquad
m_{13}=0.40,
\qquad
m_{23}=0.30.
\]

P73B recovers

\[
\gamma_1
=
\sqrt{\frac{0.48\times0.40}{0.30}}
=0.8,
\]

\[
\gamma_2
=
\sqrt{\frac{0.48\times0.30}{0.40}}
=0.6,
\]

and

\[
\gamma_3
=
\sqrt{\frac{0.40\times0.30}{0.48}}
=0.5.
\]

If the three views are independently known to be better than chance, the corresponding symmetric error rates are

\[
(\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3)
=
(0.10,0.20,0.25).
\]

The simultaneously inverted signed reliabilities

\[
(-0.8,-0.6,-0.5)
\]

produce the same three pairwise moments. This is the global label-orientation ambiguity of P73C.

The example is synthetic. It demonstrates identifiability logic, not an empirical estimate of any consciousness measure.

## 9. What P73 proves

Under the declared three-view binary symmetric measurement model, P73 proves:

1. Two heterogeneous repeated views do not identify their individual channel stabilities.
2. Three nondegenerate views identify all three stability magnitudes from pairwise moments.
3. Signed reliabilities retain exactly one global label-orientation ambiguity.
4. A better-than-chance orientation assumption converts the identified stabilities into unique symmetric error rates.
5. Hoeffding plus a union bound yields simultaneous finite-sample intervals for the three stability magnitudes.
6. An independent P73 calibration certificate can be handed to P72 with explicit confidence accounting.

---

## 10. What P73 does not prove

P73 does **not** prove that:

- the latent target \(E^\star\) is consciousness;
- self-report, behavior, a neural measure, a clinical label, or a rater is an exact view of experience;
- real target errors are symmetric;
- repeated views are conditionally independent;
- error rates are independent of the latent class, physical state, subject, time, context, or intervention;
- three arbitrary measurements are sufficient for a general multi-class or instance-dependent noise model;
- latent semantic label orientation is identifiable without an external anchor;
- a channel estimate obtained from the same data as a bridge witness can be plugged in without joint uncertainty control;
- identifiability of the measurement channel solves the physical-to-experiential bridge.

Violations of conditional independence are especially important. Shared biases, common prompting, common instrumentation, subject-specific response tendencies, or physical-state-dependent errors can make

\[
m_{ij}\ne r_i r_j.
\]

In that case the P73 identification formulas are not justified.

## 11. Relation to established latent-response mathematics

The factorization of repeated conditionally independent measurements and the broader idea of estimating observer error without observed ground truth belong to established latent-class and observer-error statistics.

Relevant context includes:

- A. P. Dawid and A. M. Skene, "Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Observer Error-Rates Using the EM Algorithm," *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series C (Applied Statistics)* 28(1), 1979, 20-28. DOI: `10.2307/2346806`.
- E. S. Allman, C. Matias, and J. A. Rhodes, "Identifiability of Parameters in Latent Structure Models with Many Observed Variables," *The Annals of Statistics* 37(6A), 2009, 3099-3132. DOI: `10.1214/09-AOS689`.

P73 does not claim invention of latent-class identifiability or Hoeffding concentration. Its repository-specific contribution is the explicit specialization needed by the P71-P72 target-validity branch:

\[
\boxed{
\text{non-circular target}
\to
\text{noisy target channel}
\to
\text{three-view stability identification}
\to
\text{finite calibration certificate}
\to
\text{measurement-aware bridge experiment}.
}
\]
