# Proposition 96: Selection-Valid Holdout Stratification

## Purpose

P95 solves the temporal-pooling failure exposed by P94 by testing predeclared regimes separately. Its remaining selection boundary is explicit: if the regime boundaries are chosen after inspecting the same certification statistic, the P95 familywise guarantee cannot simply be reused without additional accounting.

P96 closes one precise version of that gap by separating **selection** from **certification**.

A pilot-data procedure may choose the number of regimes, regime definitions, declared dependence ranges, and rational error budgets. The selected plan is then frozen. A separate certification sample, independent of the pilot-selection information, is evaluated with the P95 certificate.

The central result is:

> Arbitrarily complicated pilot selection does not require an additional alpha penalty when the selected plan is frozen and certified on independent holdout data that satisfy the selected regime-specific P94 assumptions.

The price is sample separation. Pilot observations do not count as certification observations.

---

## P96A. Pilot-selection sigma-field

Let

\[
\mathcal S
\]

denote the sigma-field generated by all information used for selection.

An arbitrary measurable selection rule may use \(\mathcal S\) to choose a finite random plan

\[
\Pi(\mathcal S)
=
\left(
B,
\{\mathcal R_b,m_b,\alpha_b\}_{b=1}^{B}
\right),
\]

where:

- \(B\) is the selected number of regimes;
- \(\mathcal R_b\) is the selected definition of regime \(b\);
- \(m_b\) is the declared within-regime finite dependence range;
- \(\alpha_b\) is the exact rational local failure budget.

The only familywise budget condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\alpha_b\le\alpha
}
\]

almost surely with respect to the pilot selection.

The selection rule may be nonlinear, discontinuous, high-dimensional, or algorithmically complex. P96 does not place a combinatorial penalty on the number of pilot rules considered before the plan is frozen.

---

## P96B. Independent certification sample

Let \(\mathcal C\) denote the certification data.

P96 requires that the information used to evaluate the P95 holdout certificate is independent of the pilot-selection information:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C\perp\!\!\!\perp\mathcal S.
}
\]

The selected plan must be frozen before any certification statistic from \(\mathcal C\) is inspected.

Conditional on \(\mathcal S\), each selected regime must satisfy the local P94 assumptions on the certification sample:

1. the certification observations assigned to regime \(b\) share one marginal four-view law \(P_b\);
2. the observations are \(m_b\)-dependent under the declared finite-range model;
3. the canonical seven-cell P92 witness is coded consistently;
4. the local error budget is \(\alpha_b\).

The regimes need not be independent of one another. P95 already uses a union bound rather than cross-regime independence.

An ordinary random split of one temporally dependent stream is **not** automatically enough to establish the pilot-holdout independence condition. The experiment or sampling design must justify it.

---

## P96C. Conditional selection-valid theorem

For each selected regime, let \(\mathcal A_b\) be the P95/P94 seven-cell confidence event for the holdout sample.

Conditional on \(\mathcal S\), the selected plan is fixed. Therefore P95 gives

\[
\Pr\left(
\bigcap_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal A_b
\middle|
\mathcal S
\right)
\ge
1-
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\alpha_b
\ge
1-\alpha
\]

almost surely.

Equivalently,

\[
\Pr\left(
\bigcup_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal A_b^c
\middle|
\mathcal S
\right)
\le
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\alpha_b
\le
\alpha.
\]

Taking expectation over the pilot information gives the unconditional bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left(
\bigcup_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal A_b^c
\right)
&=
\mathbb E\left[
\Pr\left(
\bigcup_{b=1}^{B}\mathcal A_b^c
\middle|
\mathcal S
\right)
\right]\\
&\le
\mathbb E\left[
\sum_{b=1}^{B}\alpha_b
\right]\\
&\le
\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the simultaneous holdout coverage remains at least

\[
\boxed{1-\alpha.}
\]

No extra multiplicity factor involving the complexity of the pilot segmentation rule appears.

---

## P96D. Selection-valid joint rejection

Suppose the holdout sample in at least one selected regime \(b_\star\) satisfies the P95 rejection gate:

\[
\widehat D_{b_\star,1}
\widehat D_{b_\star,2}
\widehat D_{b_\star,3}<0
\]

and

\[
\varepsilon_{b_\star}
<
\min_i\widehat r_{b_\star,i}.
\]

On the simultaneous event from P96C, the corresponding population determinant product is negative. P92 then implies

\[
P_{b_\star}\notin\mathcal M_{75}.
\]

Therefore the pilot-selected joint null

\[
\boxed{
H_0^{\mathrm{selected}}
:
P_b\in\mathcal M_{75}
\quad\text{for every regime selected by }\Pi(\mathcal S)
}
\]

is rejected with unconditional confidence at least

\[
\boxed{1-\alpha.}
\]

This is a post-selection-valid rejection statement because the certification sample is independent of the selection information.

---

## P96E. Why no selection-complexity alpha penalty appears

If the same observations were used both to search over segmentations and to certify the chosen segmentation, the selected statistic could be biased toward an extreme value. A correction would then have to account for the search procedure or use another selection-valid method.

P96 instead conditions on the pilot information. Once \(\mathcal S\) is fixed, the segmentation and budgets are ordinary fixed inputs to P95. The holdout law has not been searched by the selection rule.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{\mathrm{extra,selection}}=0
}
\]

under the P96 independence and frozen-plan assumptions.

This is not free information. The cost is that the pilot sample is unavailable for the final P95 gate.

---

## P96F. Exact-rational execution

The executable certification step remains the exact P95 gate.

For selected regime \(b\), P79 provides a rational bracket

\[
\underline L_b
\le
\log(14/\alpha_b)
\le
\overline L_b,
\]

and P95 rejects locally whenever

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(m_b+1)\overline L_b}{2n_b}
<
\left(
\min_i\widehat r_{b,i}
\right)^2.
}
\]

The pilot stage may use arbitrary numerical machinery because it does not itself issue the P96 rejection certificate. The final holdout gate can remain rational in the same sense as P94 and P95.

---

## P96G. Balanced 95 percent checkpoint

Because pilot selection complexity does not change the holdout alpha allocation, the balanced P95 threshold is inherited unchanged.

For two selected regimes, one-step dependence, and equal division of a 5 percent familywise budget,

\[
B=2,
\qquad
m=1,
\qquad
\alpha_b=\frac{1}{40}.
\]

The first mathematical holdout crossing remains

\[
\boxed{n_{\mathrm{holdout}}=3645}
\]

per regime, and the first exact denominator-24 replication remains

\[
\boxed{n_{\mathrm{holdout,exact}}=3648}
\]

per regime.

Thus the certification stage requires

\[
2\times3645=7290
\]

holdout observations for the mathematical crossing, or

\[
2\times3648=7296
\]

for the first exact replicated profile.

If the pilot used \(n_{\mathrm{pilot}}\) additional observations, total sample accounting is

\[
\boxed{
n_{\mathrm{total}}
=
n_{\mathrm{pilot}}
+
\sum_{b=1}^{B}n_b.
}
\]

For example, a 500-observation pilot gives totals 7790 and 7796 at the two balanced checkpoints above.

## P96H. What selection P96 allows

The pilot stage may choose, using pilot information only:

- how many regimes to carry forward;
- regime labels or externally meaningful definitions;
- the declared finite dependence range for each regime;
- unequal rational error budgets, provided their sum is at most \(\alpha\);
- which selected regimes receive larger or smaller certification resources.

The selection rule can be arbitrarily complicated. The theorem depends on data separation, not on a bound on the pilot algorithm's complexity.

---

## P96I. What P96 does not solve

P96 does not justify:

1. reusing certification observations to alter the selected segmentation;
2. choosing new regimes after inspecting holdout determinant signs;
3. treating an ordinary split of one dependent time series as automatically independent;
4. gradual drift inside a selected certification regime when one common marginal law is not defensible;
5. unknown or misspecified within-regime dependence ranges;
6. long-range dependence without an appropriate concentration theorem;
7. model acceptance when the holdout certificate does not reject;
8. identification of a latent variable with consciousness;
9. nonphysicality of consciousness;
10. completion of the physical-to-experiential bridge.

A natural next question is whether part of the pilot sample can be recovered without invalidating selection, for example through guarded cross-fitting, multiple independent splits, or simultaneous candidate-family accounting.
