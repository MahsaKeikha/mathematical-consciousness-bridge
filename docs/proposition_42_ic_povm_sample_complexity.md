# Proposition 42: IC-POVM finite-sample obstruction sample complexity

## Status

**Proved finite-sample sample-complexity theorem under an explicit informationally complete measurement model.** P42 instantiates the corrected P41 population design inequality with finite-outcome concentration bounds for both quantum tomography and an independently defined finite target variable.

The theorem is conditional on IID sampling, a fixed informationally complete POVM, a declared linear reconstruction map with known trace-norm stability constant, and a declared Lipschitz bridge class. It does not prove that quantum mechanics is incomplete and does not identify the target with consciousness.

---

## 1. Preparation family and population regularity gap

Let

\[
\mathcal X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}
\]

be the preparation family. For one preparation pair \(x,x'\), define

\[
d_Y=\|P_x-P_{x'}\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\qquad
d_Q=D(\rho_x,\rho_{x'}).
\]

For a declared \(L\)-Lipschitz bridge class, define the population regularity gap

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=d_Y-Ld_Q.
}
\]

A necessary starting condition for the present sufficient bound is

\[
\boxed{\Delta>0.}
\]

If \(\Delta\le0\), increasing sample size alone cannot force the P41 obstruction from this inequality.

---

## 2. Quantum measurement model

Fix an informationally complete POVM

\[
\mathcal M=\{M_1,\ldots,M_{m_Q}\}.
\]

For state \(\rho_x\), let

\[
p_x(j)=\operatorname{Tr}(M_j\rho_x).
\]

Let \(A\) be a declared linear reconstruction map satisfying

\[
A(p_x)=\rho_x
\]

for all physical probability vectors generated by the model, and assume the trace-norm stability bound

\[
\boxed{
\|A(v)\|_1\le\kappa\|v\|_1
}
\]

for all relevant zero-sum perturbation vectors \(v\).

The constant \(\kappa\) is part of the experimental design. A poorly conditioned tomography map has a larger \(\kappa\) and therefore requires more samples.

---

## 3. Empirical IC-POVM concentration

For each preparation, collect \(n_Q\) IID POVM outcomes and let \(\widehat p_x\) be the empirical frequency vector.

For one coordinate, Hoeffding gives

\[
\Pr\left(
|\widehat p_x(j)-p_x(j)|>t
\right)
\le2e^{-2n_Qt^2}.
\]

A union bound over all \(N m_Q\) preparation-outcome coordinates gives, with probability at least \(1-\alpha_Q\),

\[
\max_{x,j}|\widehat p_x(j)-p_x(j)|
\le
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{2n_Q}
}.
\]

Hence simultaneously for every preparation,

\[
\|\widehat p_x-p_x\|_1
\le
m_Q
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{2n_Q}
}.
\]

Let

\[
\widetilde\rho_x=A(\widehat p_x).
\]

Then

\[
\|\widetilde\rho_x-\rho_x\|_1
\le
\kappa m_Q
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{2n_Q}
}.
\]

If \(\widehat\rho_x\) is any trace-norm projection of \(\widetilde\rho_x\) onto the density operators, the true \(\rho_x\) is feasible, so

\[
\|\widehat\rho_x-\widetilde\rho_x\|_1
\le
\|\rho_x-\widetilde\rho_x\|_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
D(\rho_x,\widehat\rho_x)
\le
\kappa m_Q
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{2n_Q}
}.
\]

Define the simultaneous P42 quantum radius

\[
\boxed{
r_Q(n_Q)
=
\kappa m_Q
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{2n_Q}
}.
}
\]

Clipping at one is always allowed because trace distance between density operators is at most one.

---

## 4. Finite target alphabet concentration

Let the independently defined target alphabet have size \(m_Y\). For each preparation, collect \(n_Y\) IID target observations and let \(\widehat P_x\) be the empirical target distribution.

Applying the same coordinatewise Hoeffding argument over all \(N m_Y\) target coordinates gives, with probability at least \(1-\alpha_Y\),

\[
\boxed{
\|P_x-\widehat P_x\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\varepsilon_Y(n_Y)
:=
\frac{m_Y}{2}
\sqrt{
\frac{
\log(2Nm_Y/\alpha_Y)
}{2n_Y}
}
\quad\forall x.
}
\]

---

## 5. P42 finite-sample obstruction theorem

P41's corrected population sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta
>
4\varepsilon_Y(n_Y)
+4Lr_Q(n_Q).
}
\]

Therefore, on the joint confidence event, the data-dependent P41 trace-ball obstruction is positive for the chosen preparation pair.

By the union bound, the joint confidence is at least

\[
\boxed{
1-\alpha_Q-\alpha_Y.
}
\]

No independence assumption between the quantum and target data collections is required for this coverage statement.

---

## 6. Explicit sufficient sample sizes

Choose a budget split

\[
0<\lambda<1
\]

and require

\[
4\varepsilon_Y(n_Y)\le\lambda\Delta,
\]

\[
4Lr_Q(n_Q)\le(1-\lambda)\Delta.
\]

Solving the target inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
n_Y
\ge
\frac{
2m_Y^2
\log(2Nm_Y/\alpha_Y)
}{
\lambda^2\Delta^2
}.
}
\]

For \(L>0\), solving the quantum inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
n_Q
\ge
\frac{
8L^2\kappa^2m_Q^2
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{
(1-\lambda)^2\Delta^2
}.
}
\]

Integer ceilings give executable trial counts.

For the balanced choice \(\lambda=1/2\),

\[
\boxed{
n_Y
\ge
\frac{
8m_Y^2
\log(2Nm_Y/\alpha_Y)
}{
\Delta^2
},
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
n_Q
\ge
\frac{
32L^2\kappa^2m_Q^2
\log(2Nm_Q/\alpha_Q)
}{
\Delta^2
}.
}
\]

Thus both branches exhibit the expected inverse-square dependence on the population regularity gap.

---

## 7. Conditioning penalty is explicit

The quantum trial requirement scales as

\[
\boxed{n_Q=O(\kappa^2m_Q^2L^2\Delta^{-2}).}
\]

This is scientifically useful because \(\kappa\) is not hidden. Two informationally complete measurement designs with identical outcome count can have very different finite-sample performance if their reconstruction maps have different conditioning.

The theorem therefore separates three sources of difficulty:

\[
\boxed{
\text{small physical-to-target gap}
\quad+
\text{large bridge regularity constant}
\quad+
\text{ill-conditioned tomography}.
}
\]

---

## 8. Zero-Lipschitz edge case

If \(L=0\), the declared bridge class contains only target laws that are constant in the quantum-state argument. Quantum precision is then irrelevant to the regularity budget and the quantum sample-size term vanishes from the sufficient inequality.

The target branch alone must satisfy

\[
\Delta=d_Y>4\varepsilon_Y(n_Y).
\]

---

## 9. What the theorem does not establish

P42 does not show that the chosen POVM is physically complete for the real system. It does not show that the preparation model includes all relevant environmental degrees of freedom. It does not justify a particular numerical value of \(L\). It does not show that an experiential target has been validly defined.

The theorem establishes only this conditional statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{under the declared IC measurement, reconstruction stability, IID model, and Lipschitz bridge class, sufficient samples force the P41 obstruction with controlled confidence.}
}
\]

A positive certificate rejects that declared model class. It is not by itself a proof of quantum incompleteness or a nonphysical origin of the target.

---

## 10. Next theorem target

P42 uses coordinatewise Hoeffding concentration and a generic reconstruction stability constant. It is intentionally transparent but conservative.

The next natural improvements are:

1. replace coordinatewise union bounds by sharper multinomial concentration;
2. optimize the IC measurement design through \(\kappa\);
3. derive a minimax lower bound showing how much of the \(\Delta^{-2}\) scaling is unavoidable;
4. extend the theorem from fixed sample sizes to sequential or anytime-valid quantum and target confidence radii.

The strongest next theorem target is a lower-bound result: determine whether any method in the declared finite-outcome model must use order \(\Delta^{-2}\) samples to distinguish a regular bridge from a genuine regularity violation.
