# Proposition 100: Anytime-Valid Sequential E-Process

## Purpose

P99 turns one fresh cross-fitted certification round into an exact nonnegative e-value whose null expectation is at most one. That result permits distributed evidence to accumulate across folds inside one declared certification round, but it does not by itself justify repeatedly running new rounds, looking after every round, and stopping whenever the accumulated evidence happens to look decisive.

P100 closes that sequential gap under an explicit fresh-round condition.

The question is:

> Can an open-ended sequence of fresh P99 certification rounds be accumulated into one exact evidence process while permitting the next plan and betting stake to adapt to the past and permitting the stopping time itself to be data dependent?

The answer is yes when each current round remains conditionally valid given the past and its stake is predictable.

Let `E_t` be the P99 e-value from round `t`. Before inspecting round `t` certification data, choose an exact-rational stake

\[
0\le \eta_t\le 1
\]

using past information only. Define

\[
\boxed{
F_t=(1-\eta_t)+\eta_t E_t
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
M_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}F_s,
\qquad
M_0=1.
}
\]

If

\[
\mathbb E[E_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le 1,
\]

then

\[
\mathbb E[F_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le 1
\]

and `M_t` is a nonnegative supermartingale. Ville's inequality therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left(\sup_{t\ge 0}M_t\ge\frac1\alpha\right)\le\alpha.
}
\]

The first crossing of `1/alpha` is consequently an anytime-valid rejection time. Repeated inspection and optional stopping do not require a new alpha split across rounds.

P100 is a sequential extension of P99, not a relaxation of P99's scientific assumptions. Freshness, own-fold exclusion, declared dependence control, and model boundaries remain essential.

---

## P100A. Sequential filtration and declared null

Let

\[
\mathcal F_0\subseteq\mathcal F_1\subseteq\mathcal F_2\subseteq\cdots
\]

be the filtration generated by all information revealed through successive certification rounds.

At the end of round `t-1`, the analyst may use `F_{t-1}` to choose the next round's:

- P95 regime plan;
- P96/P98 selection procedure;
- P99 threshold grid and mixture weights;
- cross-fitted fold weights;
- exact-rational stake `eta_t`;
- sample allocation and other declared design quantities.

Those choices may be arbitrarily complicated functions of the past. They must be frozen before the certification data used to evaluate round `t` are inspected.

The sequential global null `H_0^seq` is the intersection of the selected P75 nulls required for every round that is actually run, together with the declared P94-P99 regularity, independence, selection-validity, and freshness assumptions needed for conditional e-value validity.

P100 does not require future rounds to be planned at time zero. It requires only that the current round's choices be predictable from the past.

---

## P100B. Conditional P99 round validity

For round `t`, let

\[
E_t\ge0
\]

be the aggregate P99 e-value computed from that round's fresh cross-fitted certification blocks.

The essential sequential assumption is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[E_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le1.
}
\]

This is stronger than merely knowing the unconditional statement `E[E_t] <= 1` separately for every fixed round.

The conditional statement is justified only when the current certification data remain valid given the accumulated past. In the implemented P100 contract this is represented explicitly by the requirement that current-round certification data be conditionally fresh given the past, together with P99's within-round block conditions.

Past information may select the current plan. It may not contain the current certification outcomes or information that invalidates their conditional null law.

## P100C. Predictable reserve-stake factor

Choose

\[
\eta_t\in[0,1]
\]

so that `eta_t` is `F_{t-1}`-measurable. Define

\[
\boxed{
F_t=(1-\eta_t)+\eta_t E_t.
}
\]

Because `eta_t` is known before the current certification data are revealed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[F_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=(1-\eta_t)+\eta_t\mathbb E[E_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\\
&\le(1-\eta_t)+\eta_t\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[F_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le1.
}
\]

The endpoints have clear interpretations:

- `eta_t = 0` skips the round for evidence accumulation, so `F_t = 1`;
- `eta_t = 1` invests the full current process in the P99 e-value, so `F_t = E_t`;
- `0 < eta_t < 1` retains a reserve that prevents an `E_t = 0` round from annihilating the entire accumulated process.

No optimality claim is made for a particular stake schedule.

## P100D. Product process is a nonnegative supermartingale

Define

\[
M_0=1,
\qquad
M_t=M_{t-1}F_t.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
M_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\bigl((1-\eta_s)+\eta_sE_s\bigr).
}
\]

Since `M_{t-1}` is nonnegative and `F_{t-1}`-measurable,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=M_{t-1}\mathbb E[F_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\\
&\le M_{t-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(M_t)_{t\ge0}\text{ is a nonnegative supermartingale under }H_0^{\mathrm{seq}}.
}
\]

This proof does not require the round e-values `E_1,E_2,...` to be mutually independent. It requires their stated conditional expectation bound given the past.

## P100E. Anytime-valid rejection by Ville's inequality

For any declared global level

\[
0<\alpha<1,
\]

Ville's inequality for nonnegative supermartingales with `M_0=1` gives

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{H_0^{\mathrm{seq}}}\!\left(
\sup_{t\ge0}M_t\ge\frac1\alpha
\right)
\le\alpha.
}
\]

Define the first crossing time

\[
T_\alpha
=
\inf\left\{t\ge1:M_t\ge\frac1\alpha\right\},
\]

with `T_alpha = infinity` if no crossing occurs.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{H_0^{\mathrm{seq}}}(T_\alpha<\infty)\le\alpha.
}
\]

Thus a valid rule is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reject }H_0^{\mathrm{seq}}
\text{ at the first round }t\text{ with }M_t\ge1/\alpha.
}
\]

The analyst may inspect `M_t` after every round and may stop at that first crossing. No additional alpha penalty is needed for the number of inspections.

## P100F. What adaptive stopping does and does not permit

P100 permits the stopping time to depend on the observed history. It also permits the next plan and next stake to depend on the past.

It does not permit the current stake to be changed after seeing the current `E_t`.

It does not permit current certification data to leak into plan selection while still being labeled fresh.

It does not turn arbitrary reused data into a new round merely by assigning a new round name.

The order of operations is therefore essential:

1. observe the history through round `t-1`;
2. freeze the round `t` plan, calibration, and stake;
3. obtain conditionally fresh certification data for round `t`;
4. compute the P99 e-value `E_t`;
5. update `M_t`;
6. inspect the anytime-valid threshold;
7. either stop or use the enlarged history to design round `t+1`.

## P100G. Exact 95 percent moderate-evidence checkpoint

Take the inherited P99 balanced design

\[
K=2,
\qquad
B=2,
\qquad
m=1,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac1{20},
\qquad
\tau=\frac1{25}.
\]

Suppose that in a given fresh P99 round exactly one of the two equally weighted folds rejects at level `1/25` and the other does not.

The P99 round e-value is then

\[
\boxed{
E_t
=
\frac12\cdot25+\frac12\cdot0
=
\frac{25}{2}
=12.5.
}
\]

Since the 95 percent single-round rejection threshold is

\[
\frac1\alpha=20,
\]

this round is individually non-decisive:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{25}{2}<20.
}
\]

Choose the predictable stake

\[
\boxed{
\eta_t=\frac12.
}
\]

The corresponding betting factor is

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_t
&=\left(1-\frac12\right)+\frac12\cdot\frac{25}{2}\\
&=\frac12+\frac{25}{4}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{27}{4}}\\
&=6.75.
\end{aligned}
\]

After one such round,

\[
M_1=\frac{27}{4}=6.75<20.
\]

After two fresh rounds with the same realized moderate evidence,

\[
\boxed{
M_2
=\left(\frac{27}{4}\right)^2
=\frac{729}{16}
=45.5625
>20.
}
\]

Therefore the first crossing occurs at round two.

This is the central P100 checkpoint:

> Two individually non-decisive P99 rounds can jointly produce an anytime-valid rejection because the evidence is accumulated through a valid sequential e-process rather than by repeatedly applying a fixed-round threshold.

## P100I. Reserve protection after a zero-evidence round

The reserve term distinguishes P100's default half-stake checkpoint from full multiplication by the current P99 e-value.

For

\[
\eta_t=\frac12
\]

and a round with

\[
E_t=0,
\]

the factor is

\[
\boxed{
F_t
=\frac12+\frac12\cdot0
=\frac12.
}
\]

Thus

\[
M_t=\frac12M_{t-1}.
\]

The process loses half its accumulated value but remains positive. By contrast, the full-stake choice `eta_t=1` would produce `F_t=0` and permanently set every later product value to zero.

The reserve therefore has a clear robustness role. P100 does not claim that `eta=1/2` is power-optimal, growth-optimal, or minimax-optimal.

## P100J. Exact arithmetic and auditable implementation

The implemented P100 certificate uses `fractions.Fraction` for:

- global alpha;
- predictable stakes;
- P99 aggregate e-values;
- per-round betting factors;
- cumulative e-process values;
- threshold comparisons;
- the 27/4 and 729/16 checkpoint values;
- inherited P99 sample-size bookkeeping.

Consequently the public checkpoint is not based on floating-point threshold rounding.

The implementation also rejects:

- a non-predictable current-round plan, calibration, or stake;
- certification data that are not declared conditionally fresh given the past;
- stakes outside `[0,1]`;
- non-Fraction stakes in the exact API;
- duplicate round identities;
- violations of P99's within-round independent certification-block contract;
- own-fold selection leakage inside a round.

## P100K. What P100 solves

P100 adds the following capability to the P92-P99 chain:

- open-ended accumulation of fresh P99 certification rounds;
- round-specific plans that may adapt to past rounds;
- round-specific predictable exact-rational stakes;
- a reserve-stake transformation that preserves conditional e-value validity;
- one nonnegative supermartingale across rounds;
- repeated inspection after every round;
- data-dependent stopping at the first global crossing;
- exact anytime-valid type-I error control through Ville's inequality;
- accumulation of several individually non-decisive P99 rounds;
- exact two-round crossing values `27/4` and `729/16`;
- exact sample accounting of `30192` and `30336` unique observations for the declared checkpoint.

---

## P100L. What P100 does not solve

P100 does not justify:

1. using current-round certification outcomes to choose the current stake;
2. using current-round certification outcomes to choose the current P99 calibration;
3. reusing the same certification observations across sequential rounds while calling the rounds fresh;
4. ignoring dependence between new certification data and the accumulated past when that dependence breaks conditional validity;
5. splitting one dependent stream into pseudo-independent rounds without a valid conditional argument;
6. misspecified P94 dependence ranges;
7. unrestricted within-regime drift beyond the P95 contract;
8. post-hoc selection rules that violate P96-P99 data separation;
9. interpreting non-rejection as evidence that the P75 family is true;
10. identifying a latent state with consciousness;
11. concluding that consciousness is nonphysical;
12. completing the physical-to-experiential bridge.

P100 is an inference theorem. It strengthens how valid evidence may be accumulated over time. It does not strengthen the ontology of the target variable or remove the scientific assumptions inherited from the earlier chain.
