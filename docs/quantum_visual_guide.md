# Quantum Figure Visual Guide

This page is the direct visual companion to the [Quantum Foundations and the Physical-to-Experiential Completeness Test](quantum_foundations_and_bridge_test.md). Every quantum figure is shown with its governing relation, a literal reading guide, the permitted takeaway, and an explicit scientific boundary. A reader should not need to infer the meaning of a curve, surface, threshold, or state-space diagram from appearance alone.

> **Scientific boundary.** These figures establish or illustrate standard quantum-mechanical structure. They do not establish that consciousness is quantum, that consciousness is a new physical dimension, or that quantum formalism is incomplete.

## QM01. Free Gaussian wavepacket spreading

![QM01. Free Gaussian wavepacket spreading](figures/quantum/qm01_free_gaussian_wavepacket.svg)

**Figure QM01. What this figure shows.** A minimum-uncertainty free Gaussian broadens because different momentum components acquire different phases.

**Governing relation.** `i hbar d_t psi = -(hbar^2/2m) d_x^2 psi`

**How to read it.** The horizontal axis is position and the plotted profiles are the position-probability density at successive times. Compare the width and peak height of the profiles: free evolution preserves total probability while the packet broadens and its peak decreases.

**Main takeaway.** Unitary free-particle evolution can spread a localized quantum state without destroying normalization.

**Scientific status.** Exact free-particle Gaussian solution in dimensionless units. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure. It does not show that consciousness is quantum, that consciousness causes wave-function collapse, that quantum mechanics is incomplete, or that consciousness is an additional physical dimension.

## QM02. Infinite square-well eigenstates

![QM02. Infinite square-well eigenstates](figures/quantum/qm02_infinite_well_eigenstates.svg)

**Figure QM02. What this figure shows.** Boundary conditions discretize the allowed stationary energies and eigenmodes.

**Governing relation.** `E_n = n^2 pi^2 hbar^2/(2mL^2), psi_n=sqrt(2/L) sin(n pi x/L)`

**How to read it.** Read position from 0 to L along the horizontal axis and compare the stationary eigenfunctions by mode number n. Each higher n adds nodes inside the well, while the associated energy increases as n squared; the wavefunction vanishes at both walls.

**Main takeaway.** Confining boundary conditions produce discrete stationary modes and an n-squared energy spectrum.

**Scientific status.** Standard quantum-mechanical eigenvalue problem. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure. ## QM04. Heisenberg uncertainty frontier

![QM04. Heisenberg uncertainty frontier](figures/quantum/qm04_uncertainty_frontier.svg)

**Figure QM04. What this figure shows.** Gaussian minimum-uncertainty states saturate the boundary while squeezing trades position variance against momentum variance.

**Governing relation.** `Delta x Delta p >= hbar/2`

**How to read it.** The axes are position uncertainty and momentum uncertainty. The boundary curve marks Delta x Delta p = hbar/2; points on it are minimum-uncertainty Gaussian states. Moving along the boundary squeezes one uncertainty only by enlarging the conjugate uncertainty, and the physically allowed region lies on or above the bound.

**Main takeaway.** Squeezing redistributes conjugate uncertainty; it does not evade the Heisenberg product bound.

**Scientific status.** Standard quantum-mechanical uncertainty relation. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure. ## QM05. Bloch-sphere state geometry

![QM05. Bloch-sphere state geometry](figures/quantum/qm05_bloch_sphere.svg)

**Figure QM05. What this figure shows.** A qubit density matrix maps to a Bloch vector; pure states lie on the unit sphere and mixed states lie inside it.

**Governing relation.** `rho = (I + r dot sigma)/2; pure states satisfy |r|=1`

**How to read it.** Treat the sphere as the geometric state space of one qubit. The north and south poles represent the computational-basis pure states, equatorial directions represent coherent superpositions, and the state vector gives the Bloch coordinates. Pure states lie on the surface; mixed states would lie inside the sphere rather than on it.

**Main takeaway.** A qubit state has a compact geometric representation in which purity corresponds to distance from the sphere center.

**Scientific status.** Standard finite-dimensional quantum-state geometry. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure. It does not show that consciousness is quantum, that consciousness causes wave-function collapse, that quantum mechanics is incomplete, or that consciousness is an additional physical dimension.

## QM06. Born probabilities

![QM06. Born probabilities](figures/quantum/qm06_born_probabilities.svg)

**Figure QM06. What this figure shows.** Measurement probabilities are squared projection amplitudes and sum to one for a complete projective measurement.

**Governing relation.** `P(0)=cos^2(theta/2), P(1)=sin^2(theta/2)`

**How to read it.** Follow the state angle theta and compare the two complementary outcome probabilities. As theta rotates the qubit from one basis pole toward the other, P(0) decreases as cos squared(theta/2) while P(1) increases as sin squared(theta/2); the two curves always sum to one.

**Main takeaway.** Quantum measurement probabilities arise from squared projection amplitudes and obey normalization.

**Scientific status.** Standard Born rule for a pure qubit. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure. It does not show that consciousness is quantum, that consciousness causes wave-function collapse, that quantum mechanics is incomplete, or that consciousness is an additional physical dimension.

## QM10. Von Neumann entropy

![QM10. Von Neumann entropy](figures/quantum/qm10_von_neumann_entropy.svg)

**Figure QM10. What this figure shows.** For eigenvalues p and 1-p, entropy is zero for a pure state and one bit for a maximally mixed qubit.

**Governing relation.** `S(rho)=-Tr(rho log2 rho)`

**How to read it.** Read one eigenvalue p horizontally; the other is 1-p. The vertical axis is entropy in bits. Entropy is zero at p=0 or p=1 because the state is pure, and it reaches one bit at p=1/2 because a qubit with equal eigenvalues is maximally mixed.

**Main takeaway.** For a qubit, spectral balance maximizes von Neumann entropy and spectral certainty minimizes it.

**Scientific status.** Standard quantum-information entropy. This is a standard quantum-mechanics or quantum-information figure.
