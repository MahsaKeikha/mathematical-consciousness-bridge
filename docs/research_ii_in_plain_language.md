# Research II in plain language

## The question

Research II asks a narrower question than "What is consciousness?"

It asks:

> **If a theory says that a particular physical or computational description contains everything needed for a target related to experience, what would have to be true for that claim to survive a serious scientific test?**

The project does not begin by assuming that consciousness is a quantum state, an information quantity, a causal graph, a computation, or a new physical dimension. It begins with a declared description and asks whether that description is actually sufficient for an independently defined target.

## A simple example

Imagine two experimental cases that are identical according to the physical description a theory says is sufficient.

If the independently measured target is also necessarily the same, the sufficiency claim remains possible.

If the target differs in a way the declared model cannot produce, then that declared description is not sufficient for that target.

That conclusion is deliberately limited. It does not prove that consciousness is nonphysical. The physical description may have omitted important variables, used the wrong system boundary, lost relevant temporal information, or relied on an inadequate measurement model.

## The testing sequence

| Step | Plain-language question | What Research II contributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Declare the description** | What information does the theory actually claim is enough? | Representation, causal, scale, temporal, and quantum-operational definitions make the tested descriptor explicit. |
| **2. Define the target independently** | Was the target measured independently, or was it built from the same variables being tested? | P71-P76 formalize non-circular target provenance, target-measurement noise, identifiability, and model adequacy. |
| **3. Test sufficiency** | Does the target depend only on the declared description? | P19 gives the core deterministic and stochastic sufficiency criteria and exact non-factorization logic. |
| **4. Test the complete model family** | Can the full declared model, not just one fitted parameter point, reproduce the observed law? | P77-P92 build certified model-family separation, culminating in an exact nonlinear distance result for the tracked synthetic example. |
| **5. Carry the result into real finite data** | Could the apparent incompatibility be sampling noise, dependence, or drift? | P20-P24 and P93-P95 add finite-data confidence, dependence control, and predeclared drift handling. |
| **6. Protect adaptive analysis** | Does choosing models or looking repeatedly invalidate the evidence? | P96-P100 add independent holdout selection, simultaneous candidate protection, cross-fitting, e-values, and anytime-valid sequential evidence. |

## What has actually been completed

The repository now contains **100 proposition-level results through P100**. They do not form one hundred independent claims about consciousness. They form a linked mathematical and computational testing program.

The completed record includes:

- exact conditions for when a target can factor through a declared physical descriptor;
- finite-sample versions of those tests;
- descriptor-refinement logic so rejection of a coarse description does not end the analysis;
- scale-aware and quantum-operational specializations;
- target-provenance checks that prevent circular definitions from masquerading as evidence;
- target-measurement identifiability and adequacy tests;
- exact and certified separation from a continuous declared model family;
- finite-data rejection under dependence and predeclared drift;
- protection against data-dependent model or regime selection;
- cross-fitted evidence aggregation;
- anytime-valid accumulation across fresh certification rounds at P100.

The exact synthetic P92 example proves a global distance of `1/24` from the complete tracked P75 model family. P93-P100 then develop increasingly realistic rules for converting such a population-level separation into valid finite, selected, distributed, and sequential evidence.

## What P100 means without the statistics jargon

Suppose one carefully protected test round gives some evidence against a declared model, but not enough to cross the final threshold.

A common mistake would be to keep collecting and checking new data until the result happens to look significant, without accounting for the repeated looks.

P100 gives a valid way to accumulate evidence across genuinely fresh certification rounds. The next round may be designed using what was learned previously, but its certification data cannot leak into its own plan. Under those conditions, the evidence may be inspected after every round and the test may stop at the first valid threshold crossing.

## What Research II does not establish

Research II does **not** establish that:

- consciousness is nonphysical;
- consciousness is quantum;
- a latent statistical variable is consciousness;
- rejection of one descriptor rules out every richer physical descriptor;
- failure to reject a model validates that model;
- the physical-to-experiential bridge has been completed.

A rejection is always relative to a declared model and its assumptions. A non-rejection means only that the current evidence did not separate from that model under the current test.
