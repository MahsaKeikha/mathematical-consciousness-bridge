# Conscious-State Measurement Atlas

This atlas organizes empirical states and measurement interfaces without reducing consciousness to behavioral responsiveness, one neural marker, or one scalar index.

![Conscious-state measurement and dissociation map](figures/conscious_state_measurement_map.svg)

---

# 1. Why dissociations matter

A central empirical difficulty is that the following can dissociate:

\[
\boxed{
\text{behavioral responsiveness},
\quad
\text{subjective report},
\quad
\text{neural command response},
\quad
\text{perturbational complexity},
\quad
\text{environmental connectedness}.
}
\]

A bridge theory should therefore predict relations among multiple evidence channels rather than using one channel as the definition of the target experiential state.

---

# 2. Sleep and dreaming

Sleep provides a natural dissociation between environmental responsiveness and reported experience. Siclari et al. used high-density EEG with serial awakenings to compare neural activity associated with dreaming and no-experience reports.

**Primary source:** Francesca Siclari et al., "The neural correlates of dreaming," *Nature Neuroscience* 20 (2017): 872-878. DOI: `10.1038/nn.4545`.

**Repository role:** motivates state comparisons where behavioral responsiveness is reduced but retrospective experiential evidence remains available.

---

# 3. Anesthetic unresponsiveness

Behavioral unresponsiveness under anesthesia is not a uniform experiential condition across agents. Sarasso et al. compared propofol, xenon, and ketamine and reported differences in perturbational complexity together with different post-anesthetic experiential reports.

**Primary source:** Simone Sarasso et al., "Consciousness and Complexity during Unresponsiveness Induced by Propofol, Xenon, and Ketamine," *Current Biology* 25(23) (2015): 3099-3105. DOI: `10.1016/j.cub.2015.10.014`.

**Repository role:** motivates agent-specific perturbation-response analysis rather than equating unresponsiveness with one physical state.

---

# 4. Perturbational complexity

Casali et al. introduced a TMS-EEG perturbational complexity approach intended to characterize distributed cortical responses independently of ordinary sensory stimulation and overt behavior.

**Primary source:** Adenauer G. Casali et al., "A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior," *Science Translational Medicine* 5(198) (2013): 198ra105. DOI: `10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294`.

The repository does not identify its causal-structure candidate with PCI. The shared scientific idea is that controlled perturbation reveals aspects of system organization hidden from passive observation.

---

# 5. Acute brain injury and covert command response

Claassen et al. used EEG to detect brain activation associated with spoken motor commands in some clinically unresponsive patients with acute brain injury.

**Primary source:** Jan Claassen et al., "Detection of Brain Activation in Unresponsive Patients with Acute Brain Injury," *New England Journal of Medicine* 380 (2019): 2497-2505. DOI: `10.1056/NEJMoa1812757`.

**Repository role:** demonstrates why overt motor behavior and neural evidence should remain separate measurement channels in the bridge architecture.

---

# 6. Multimodal evidence vector

A future empirical bridge test can represent evidence as

\[
\boxed{
E
=
(E_{\mathrm{behavior}},
E_{\mathrm{report}},
E_{\mathrm{EEG}},
E_{\mathrm{MEG}},
E_{\mathrm{fMRI}},
E_{\mathrm{perturbation}},
E_{\mathrm{physiology}}).
}
\]

A candidate theory \(\mathfrak T_j\) should then specify a probability law

\[
\boxed{
P_{\mathfrak T_j}(E\mid p,\pi)
}
\]

under physical state \(p\) and protocol \(\pi\).

This brings the empirical state problem directly into Propositions 2-4:

\[
\Delta_\Pi(\mathfrak T_i,\mathfrak T_j;p)
=
\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}
\left\|
P_i^{\pi,p}-P_j^{\pi,p}
\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]

The scientific question becomes not "which state label is consciousness?" but "which theory predicts the observed pattern of dissociations across modalities?"

---

# 7. Connection to intervention-resolved causal structure

The repository physical candidate begins from

\[
P_p^{u,\tau}
=
\mathcal L(Y_{t+\tau}^{V}\mid do(u),p).
\]

A clinical or experimental implementation could estimate response laws across states such as wakefulness, sleep, anesthetic conditions, or disorders of consciousness, then compare:

\[
\mathcal G_p,
\qquad
\mathcal A_p,
\qquad
\mathcal K_p,
\qquad
V_{0:T},
\qquad
\chi_{A|B},
\]

while separately recording report and behavioral evidence.

This architecture permits physical-structure hypotheses to be tested without making report, responsiveness, or any single complexity measure the bridge definition.

---

# 8. Source and interpretation discipline

| Evidence source | What it can support | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| behavioral command following | overt responsiveness | absence of experience when missing |
| retrospective report | evidence of remembered experience | complete physical mechanism |
| EEG/MEG/fMRI activation | neural correlates / task-related processing | sufficient bridge condition |
| perturbational response | physical response organization | experiential identity |
| clinical diagnosis | state classification under clinical criteria | complete experiential equivalence class |

The bridge problem remains the task of connecting these measurement relations to a formal experiential domain under explicit and falsifiable principles.
